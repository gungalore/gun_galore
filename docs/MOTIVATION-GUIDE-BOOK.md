# Firearm Licence Motivation Guide Book

Sections 13, 14, 15, 16 and 24 of the Firearms Control Act 60 of 2000

Version 1.0, 9 September 2026. Written for the All Outdoor motivation generator and for the writing model (Gemini) that produces the wording. Every rule in this book is either law (with the section or regulation cited), documented SAPS or DFO practice (marked as practice), or a house rule (marked as house rule). Nothing here is legal advice; it is a working manual built from the Act, the Regulations, the SAPS 271 form, association guidance and a corpus of motivations that were lodged and approved.

---

## Part 0. How to read and use this book

### 0.1 Who reads what

The book has two readers.

The generator (code) reads Parts 1 to 4, 7, 9, 10 and 11. Those parts decide whether a motivation may be written at all, what facts the writer is given, which annexures exist, how they are lettered, what the applicant must bring, and what is checked before the pack is released.

The writer (the language model) reads Parts 5, 6 and 8. Those parts say what each section of the document must argue, in what order, how long, in what voice, and what words are forbidden. Part 5 is the chapter for the licence section applied for; the writer gets only the chapter that applies, never the whole book.

### 0.2 The one principle

A motivation is a signed, first-person letter from the applicant to the Registrar of Firearms, lodged through the Designated Firearms Officer (DFO), that proves a statutory test with facts the annexures can verify. It is not an essay, not a brochure and not a plea. Every sentence either states a fact that a document in the pack proves, or explains why that fact satisfies the section applied under. Anything else is padding and padding is what makes a DFO stop reading.

### 0.3 Terms used in this book

- The Act: the Firearms Control Act 60 of 2000, consolidated with the amendments that have actually commenced (Act 43 of 2003; the 21 commenced sections of Act 28 of 2006). Sections 13, 14, 15 and 24 have never been amended; s 16(1)(c) was substituted by Act 43 of 2003 s 4 (in force) and the later deletion of that paragraph by Act 28 of 2006 s 11 never commenced, so the text quoted in Part 2 is the current law for all five.
- The Regulations: the Firearms Control Regulations, 2004 (GN R.345, GG 26156).
- Registrar: the Registrar of Firearms (the Central Firearms Register, CFR).
- DFO: the Designated Firearms Officer at the police station for the area where the applicant ordinarily resides. Only that DFO may receive the application (reg 13(4)(a) read with the reg 1 definition of "relevant Designated Firearms Officer"; s 9(1) for competency applications; s 24(2)(b) for renewals).
- Competency certificate: the certificate under s 10 that says which firearm types the holder has been tested on. Without the relevant one no licence may be issued (s 6(2)).
- Proficiency: the training Statement of Results (PFTC, or older SASSETA or POSLEC) that lists the unit standards passed. It is the evidence behind the competency certificate, not a substitute for it.
- Dedicated status: the standing granted by an accredited hunting association or sports-shooting organisation to a member who has completed its course and keeps up its activity reporting. Only s 16 needs it.
- Endorsement: an association's letter or form stating that a specific firearm (by serial) is suitable for the stated purpose. It is association practice, not a statutory document. The statutory s 16 document is the chairperson's sworn statement of registered membership (s 16(2)).
- Battery: the firearms already licensed to the applicant. SAPS 271 section G item 2 lists them all and the motivation must agree with that list serial for serial.
- Pack: the bound set lodged at the counter: SAPS 271 (or 518(a) for a renewal), the motivation, the PAJA letter, the annexures in lettered order, and the applicant's own checklist page which is removed before lodging.

### 0.4 What is law and what is practice

The Act and Regulations require surprisingly little on paper: the prescribed form completed in black ink by the applicant personally (reg 13(2), (3)), a full set of fingerprints taken at the station by the DFO or designated personnel (s 6(1)(a), reg 13(9)), a certified copy of the ID photo page (reg 13(4)(b)), proof of fee payment (reg 13(6)), the relevant competency certificate in the applicant's possession (s 6(2)), a safe that meets reg 86 (reg 13(12)), and for s 16 the chairperson's sworn statement (s 16(2)). The written motivation itself rests on SAPS 271 item G.61 ("Motivation of purpose for which the firearm is required, applicable to all types of applications") and on reg 13(17) (the Registrar may require any further information needed to exercise discretion). Everything else in a pack (safe photographs, proof of address, proficiency copy, licence copies, association papers, endorsement, activity reports) is DFO practice. The book never tells an applicant that the law requires something a station merely asks for, but it does tell the applicant to bring it, because a station that asks and does not get it sends the applicant home.

## Part 1. Absolute rules

These apply to every document for every section. A pack that breaks one of them is not released.

1. Truth only. Every fact in the motivation comes from the applicant's answers or from a document in the pack. The writer never invents a date, place, distance, figure, qualification, incident, membership, discipline, species, farm, employer, security measure or firearm specification. Where a fact is missing the writer says less; it never fills the gap.
2. First person, applicant's voice, no service name anywhere in the lodged pack. No "prepared by", no footer brand, no "we". The applicant signs it as their own letter.
3. English, plain South African usage: licence (noun), license (verb), calibre, centre-fire, self-defence, authorisation, favourable, metres, programme. Afrikaans species and equipment names the applicant used are kept as given.
4. No outcome language. Never "should succeed", "will be approved", "meets the threshold", "the Registrar must grant", "favourable consideration". The document ends by asking for the licence, which is a request, not a prediction.
5. Quote the Act only from the verified text in Part 2, only the section applied under, only the elements the applicant must satisfy, and under each quoted element put the fact that satisfies it. Provisions like s 13(4), s 14(6), s 15(4) and s 16(3) ("may be used where it is safe to use the firearm and for a lawful purpose") are permissions attached to the licence, not tests, and are never "satisfied".
6. Section discipline. A s 13 or s 14 document contains no hunting, sport, competition, discipline, reloading or collecting words, except in a sentence describing a firearm already held under s 15, 16 or 17. A s 15 or s 16 document contains no self-defence, protection, carry, backup or home-defence words, except in a sentence describing a firearm already held under s 13 or 14. A firearm held under s 17 is described as collected, never as used.
7. Held firearms are described only from their licence cards and stated uses. The section a held firearm is licensed under comes from its card. Its purpose comes from the applicant's stated use or an endorsement naming that serial. Where neither exists the writer names the firearm and its calibre and says nothing about what it is for.
8. No product copy. No manufacturer history, no ballistics tables, no magazine capacity, no "stopping power", no "platform", no comparison of models, no pasted articles or discipline rules. The firearm is described in the fewest sentences that connect it to the purpose (Part 6).
9. No competency expiry date in prose. The competency is cited by number and type and described as valid. The SAPS competency card does not normally print an expiry (practice); any date is derived and a wrong one is fatal.
10. No statement about a clean record. SAPS 271 items G.62 to G.67 are the declaration and SAPS verifies them. The motivation does not say "I have no criminal record". If the applicant answered Yes to any of those items, the motivation deals with it plainly in the record section (Part 5).
11. Every annexure citation points to a letter that exists in the pack, using the exact title in the index. Most sentences carry no citation; a citation follows a sentence the annexure actually proves.
12. Numbers agree everywhere. The firearm's type, make, model, calibre and serial, the applicant's name and ID number, the section applied under, the competency number, the association name and membership number, and every held firearm's serial and section must be identical on the cover, in the body, on the PAJA letter, in the annexure index, on the checklist and on the SAPS 271.
13. Nothing is signed before the counter. The SAPS form is signed in front of the DFO. The motivation and PAJA letter are signed by the applicant before lodging, at home, after reading them.

## Part 2. The law the generator must get right

This part is the legal spine. The generator uses it for gating (Part 3) and the writer uses the quoted text for the statutory section of the document (Part 5). All quotations are from the Act as it stands (ss 13, 14, 15 and 24 as enacted; s 16 as amended by Act 43 of 2003).

### 2.1 Definitions (s 1)

- "handgun" means a pistol or revolver which can be held in and discharged with one hand.
- "semi-automatic" means self-loading but not capable of discharging more than one shot with a single depression of the trigger.
- "fully automatic" means capable of discharging more than one shot with a single depression of the trigger.
- "restricted firearm" means any firearm contemplated in section 14(1).
- "dedicated hunter" means a person who actively participates in hunting activities and who is a member of an accredited hunting association.
- "dedicated sports person" means a person who actively participates in sports-shooting and who is a member of an accredited sports-shooting organisation.
- "occasional hunter" means any person who, from time to time, participates in hunting activities but who is not a member of an accredited hunting association.
- "occasional sports person" means any person who, from time to time, participates in sports-shooting but who is not a member of an accredited sports-shooting organisation.
- A muzzle loading firearm needs no licence under any section since 10 January 2011 (s 3(2)) and has been excluded from the definition of "firearm" since 1 March 2012 (s 1); it needs only its own competency (s 10(1)(a)(iA)). This book does not cover it.
- Prohibited firearms (s 4: fully automatic firearms and the rest of that list) can never be licensed under ss 13 to 16.

Note on s 15 and association members (house rule, chosen 9 September 2026): the s 1 definitions of "occasional" still contain the words "but who is not a member of an accredited association"; the 2006 amendment that would have removed them never commenced. In practice associations issue s 15 endorsements to members and DFOs accept them. The generator writes s 15 motivations for association members. The motivation may say "I am a member of" the association; it never says "dedicated" anywhere in a s 15 document, never cites dedicated status, and never attaches a dedicated status certificate. The applicant checklist carries the note in Part 10.6.

### 2.2 Section 13. Licence to possess firearm for self-defence

> 13. (1) A firearm in respect of which a licence may be issued in terms of this section is any
> (a) shotgun which is not fully or semi-automatic; or
> (b) handgun which is not fully automatic.
> (2) The Registrar may issue a licence under this section to any natural person who
> (a) needs a firearm for self-defence; and
> (b) cannot reasonably satisfy that need by means other than the possession of a firearm.
> (3) No person may hold more than one licence issued in terms of this section.
> (4) A firearm in respect of which a licence has been issued in terms of this section may be used where it is safe to use the firearm and for a lawful purpose.

Elements the applicant must satisfy: 13(1) (the firearm is a handgun that is not fully automatic, or a manually operated shotgun); 13(2)(a) (a need for self-defence, argued from the applicant's own circumstances); 13(2)(b) (the need cannot reasonably be met by other means, argued from what is already in place and where it stops); 13(3) (the applicant holds no other s 13 licence, or is re-licensing the one they hold). Validity five years (s 27).

### 2.3 Section 14. Licence to possess restricted firearm for self-defence

> 14. (1) For purposes of this Act, a restricted firearm is any
> (a) semi-automatic rifle or shotgun, which cannot readily be converted into a fully automatic firearm; or
> (b) firearm declared by the Minister, by notice in the Gazette, to be a restricted firearm.
> (4) The Registrar may issue a licence in terms of this section to any natural person who shows that a firearm contemplated in section 13(1) will not provide sufficient protection, and who submits reasonable information to motivate the need for a restricted firearm for self-defence purposes.
> (5) No person may hold more than one licence issued in terms of this section.
> (6) A firearm in respect of which a licence has been issued in terms of this section may be used where it is safe to use the firearm and for a lawful purpose.

Elements: 14(1)(a) (the firearm is a semi-automatic rifle or shotgun that cannot readily be converted); 14(4) first limb (a s 13 firearm, that is a handgun or manual shotgun, will not provide sufficient protection, argued from the specific circumstances); 14(4) second limb (reasonable information motivating the need for a restricted firearm); 14(5) (no other s 14 licence held). Validity two years (s 27). This is the only section of the five where the Act itself uses the word "motivate". SAPS 271 section K (the DFO's report on urban or rural residence, distance to neighbours and police station, crime-rated area, high-risk work or residence, dedicated or collector status and the number of firearms held) is completed only for s 14, so the s 14 motivation must give the DFO every fact section K asks for.

### 2.4 Section 15. Licence to possess firearm for occasional hunting and sports-shooting

> 15. (1) A firearm in respect of which a licence may be issued in terms of this section is any
> (a) handgun which is not fully automatic;
> (b) rifle or shotgun which is not fully or semi-automatic; or
> (c) barrel, frame or receiver of a handgun, rifle or shotgun contemplated in paragraph (a) or (b),
> and which is not a restricted firearm.
> (2) The Registrar may issue a licence in terms of this section to any natural person who is an occasional hunter or occasional sports person.
> (3) (a) Subject to paragraphs (b), (c) and (d), no person may hold more than four licences issued in terms of this section.
> (b) If a person holds a licence issued in terms of section 13, he or she may only hold three licences issued in terms of this section.
> (c) A person may not hold more than one licence in respect of a handgun contemplated in subsection (1)(a).
> (d) If a person contemplated in paragraph (a) holds any additional licences contemplated in section 12 in respect of a firearm contemplated in this section and section 13, the number of licences which that person may hold must be reduced by the number of such additional licences held.
> (4) A firearm in respect of which a licence has been issued in terms of this section may be used where it is safe to use the firearm and for a lawful purpose.

Elements: 15(1) (type and action); 15(2) (the applicant is in fact an occasional hunter or occasional sports person, shown by real participation); 15(3) (the count after this licence stays within four, or three where a s 13 is held, and only one s 15 handgun). There is no "need" test in s 15; the motivation is evidentiary. Validity ten years (s 27).

### 2.5 Section 16. Licence to possess firearm for dedicated hunting and dedicated sports-shooting

> 16. (1) A firearm in respect of which a licence may be issued in terms of this section is any
> (a) handgun which is not fully automatic;
> (b) rifle or shotgun which is not fully automatic;
> (c) semi-automatic shotgun manufactured to fire no more than five shots in succession without having to be reloaded; or
> (d) barrel, frame or receiver of a handgun, rifle or shotgun contemplated in paragraph (a), (b) or (c).
> (2) The Registrar may issue a licence in terms of this section to any natural person who is a dedicated hunter or dedicated sports person if the application is accompanied by a sworn statement or solemn declaration from the chairperson of an accredited hunting association or sports-shooting organisation, or someone delegated in writing by him or her, stating that the applicant is a registered member of that association.
> (3) A firearm in respect of which a licence has been issued in terms of this section may be used where it is safe to use the firearm and for a lawful purpose.

Elements: 16(1) (type; a semi-automatic rifle is allowed; a semi-automatic shotgun only if manufactured for five shots or fewer; paragraph (c) as substituted by Act 43 of 2003 is in force and its deletion in Act 28 of 2006 never commenced, so the limit is law); 16(2) first part (the applicant is a dedicated hunter or dedicated sports person: active participation plus membership of an accredited association); 16(2) second part (the chairperson's sworn statement or solemn declaration of registered membership accompanies the application). There is no numeric cap and no "need" test, but the Registrar's discretion ("may") is exercised on the motivation, so the document must still show why this firearm serves the dedicated activity. Validity ten years (s 27).

Section 16A (professional hunting) is outside this book.

### 2.6 Section 24. Renewal of firearm licences

> 24. (1) The holder of a licence issued in terms of this Chapter who wishes to renew the licence must at least 90 days before the date of expiry of the licence apply to the Registrar for its renewal.
> (2) The application must be
> (a) accompanied by such information as may be prescribed; and
> (b) delivered to the Designated Firearms Officer responsible for the area in which the applicant ordinarily resides or in which the applicant's business is, as the case may be.
> (3) No application for the renewal of a licence may be granted unless the applicant shows that he or she has continued to comply with the requirements for the licence in terms of this Act.
> (4) If an application for the renewal of a licence has been lodged within the period provided for in subsection (1), the licence remains valid until the application is decided.

A renewal is always a renewal of a s 13, 14, 15 or 16 licence. The section of the licence being renewed is read off the licence card and drives the whole document: the s 24(3) test is the original section's test applied to the present. Form: SAPS 518(a), not SAPS 271. The competency certificate tied to that licence is renewed at the same time under s 10A(1) on a sworn declaration of continued s 9(2) compliance, with no re-testing (s 10A(7)). Timing: lodged at least 90 days before expiry; the acknowledgement of receipt (SAPS 523) is court proof of compliance (reg 13(13)).

### 2.7 Validity and counts at a glance

| Section | Firearm types | Cap | Validity | Form |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | Handgun (not fully auto); shotgun (manual only) | One s 13 licence at a time | 5 years | SAPS 271, D.3.1 |
| 14 | Semi-auto rifle or shotgun, not readily convertible | One s 14 licence at a time | 2 years | SAPS 271, D.3.2, plus DFO section K |
| 15 | Handgun (not fully auto); manual rifle or shotgun; not a restricted firearm | Four; three if a s 13 is held; only one handgun; less any s 12 additional licences | 10 years | SAPS 271, D.3.3 |

Note: s 12 additional licences (a co-resident holding a licence for the same firearm) are outside this book except that they count against the s 15 ceiling.

### 2.8 Competency to firearm matching

No licence may be issued to a person who is not in possession of the relevant competency certificate (s 6(2)). The certificate must specify all the relevant tests successfully completed (s 10(1)(b)); the accredited trainer records the type of firearm, "a handgun, rifle or shotgun including the action of the firearm", for which the practical test was passed (reg 7(2)(d)). In practice the CFR records a competency by type and, for rifles, by action (the SAPS 271 G.1.4 ticks show only Handgun / Rifle / Shotgun; the action split sits on the certificate and the CFR record). The unit standards are SAQA numbers used by PFTC-accredited trainers, not statute; older SASSETA and POSLEC certificates remain valid.

| Firearm applied for | Competency endorsement needed | Unit standard | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revolver | Handgun | 119649 | |
| Semi-automatic pistol | Handgun | 119649 | A pistol is a handgun. There is no separate self-loading handgun endorsement. |
| Bolt-action, lever-action, pump-action, straight-pull, single-shot or falling-block rifle | Rifle, manually operated | 119651 (SAQA title "handle and use of a manually operated rifle or carbine"; association guides call it "bolt-action rifle") | A lever action is manually operated; it is not "self-loading" (s 1). |
| Semi-automatic rifle or carbine, including a pistol-calibre carbine | Rifle or carbine, self-loading | 119650 | A manual-rifle endorsement does not cover it. A handgun endorsement does not cover a carbine. |
| Break, pump, bolt or lever shotgun | Shotgun | 119652 | |
| Semi-automatic shotgun | Shotgun | 119652 | One shotgun unit standard covers both actions. Where the CFR printout carries a "manually operated shotgun" qualifier the applicant should have it corrected at the DFO before lodging. |
| Combination gun (rifle and shotgun barrels) | Rifle of the relevant action, and shotgun | 119651 or 119650, plus 119652 | Not addressed in the Act; a DFO reads reg 7(2)(d) literally. |
| Any firearm | plus knowledge of the Act | 117705 | Mandatory for every competency (s 9(2)(q)). |

The licence section does not change the competency category. A handgun for s 13, s 15 or s 16 needs the same handgun endorsement.

If the applicant's competency does not cover the type and action applied for, the application cannot succeed as lodged: s 6(2) bars the licence and under reg 13(10) the DFO may decline to issue an acknowledgement of receipt because the application is not accompanied by all the required documentation. The applicant must first obtain a further competency certificate on SAPS 517(a) (s 9(6)); in practice the DFO asks for the Statement of Results for the new unit standard, and reg 14(9) lets an applicant who already passed that training submit that proof instead of training again. The generator refuses to write the motivation and says exactly this (Part 3).

Competency validity: since 2011 a possession competency "remains valid for the same period of validity as ... the licence to which the competency certificate relates" (s 10(2)). The card does not normally print an expiry (practice). The generator may hold an expiry for its own gate (Part 3) but the writer never prints one (Part 1, rule 9). SAPS 271 G.1.4 to G.1.7 carry the type ticks, number, date of issue and expiry; the expiry box is filled from the CFR printout if the applicant has one, otherwise left for the DFO.

### 2.9 Other provisions the document relies on

- s 6(1)(a): application on the prescribed form with a full set of fingerprints; the DFO or designated personnel take them at the station (reg 13(9)). This is why nobody else can lodge the pack.
- s 9(2): the fit-and-proper criteria for a competency. The motivation does not re-argue them; SAPS 271 G.62 to G.67 and the CFR do that.
- s 11(1): a separate licence for each firearm (s 12 allows a co-resident an additional licence for the same firearm).
- s 23: the firearm must bear a serial number (SAPS 271 E.1.7 to E.1.12).
- s 120(9)(f): a false statement in an application is an offence. It is printed on the SAPS 271 declarations and on association endorsement forms.
- reg 13(3): the form is completed in black ink by the applicant personally.
- reg 13(4)(b): a certified copy of the ID photo page. The only certification the Regulations require of a natural person.
- reg 13(6): proof of fee payment attached.
- reg 13(7): anyone giving a character recommendation must state whether the applicant is fit and proper, of stable mental condition and not inclined to violence, and not dependent on any intoxicating or narcotic substance. (The pack does not include references by house rule; if an applicant adds one, it must contain these three points.)
- reg 13(12) read with reg 86(1): the Registrar may only issue a licence to a person whose safe custody complies: a safe or strongroom conforming to SABS 953-1 and 953-2. SAPS 271 G.68 to G.69 ask about the safe; the DFO inspects it (SAPS 271 L.1.2).
- reg 13(16): a recent colour photograph, full front view, neutral background, 32 mm by 40 mm on request; SAPS practice asks for two passport photographs.
- reg 89: any police official taking an administrative decision that may detrimentally affect a person must record written reasons when the decision is made, sign and date them, and notify the person in writing of the decision, the reasons, and the date and place. This is the basis of the PAJA letter (Part 9).
- reg 91(1)(a) and (4): an appeal to the Appeal Board within 90 days of the decision, attaching the reg 89(c) notification.
- PAJA s 3(2) and Constitution s 33: procedurally fair administrative action, prior notice and an opportunity to make representations.

### 2.10 Version discipline for citations

Sections 13, 14, 15, 16 (as it stands), 24 and 120(9)(f) and regulations 13, 86, 89 and 91 may be cited flatly. Sections 10(2), 10A and 27 are cited in their amended form. Never cite a provision of Act 28 of 2006 that is not in force (in particular never say the five-shot shotgun limit was removed, never say "fit and proper" is defined, never say fingerprints are only "as the Registrar may require", and never say the occasional-hunter definition no longer excludes association members). Fees and form numbers change; the document names the form and the checklist says "confirm the fee at the station".

## Part 3. Pre-writing gates

The generator runs these before any wording is produced. There are two classes.

### 3.1 Hard blocks (the Registrar cannot lawfully issue the licence; do not write)

| Check | Rule | Message to the applicant |
|---|---|---|
| Firearm type not permitted by the section | s 13: no rifle, no semi-auto shotgun. s 14: only a semi-auto rifle or shotgun. s 15: no semi-auto rifle or shotgun, nothing restricted. s 16: nothing fully automatic. All: no prohibited firearm. | Name the section, quote the subsection, and name the section that would work (a semi-auto shotgun for self-defence is s 14; a semi-auto rifle for sport is s 16; a manual rifle for occasional hunting is s 15). |
| No competency certificate, or competency does not cover the type and action | s 6(2), s 10(1)(b), reg 7(2)(d). Table 2.8. | "Your competency certificate covers X. This firearm needs Y. Apply for a further competency on SAPS 517(a) with the Statement of Results for unit standard N first." |

### 3.2 Warnings (the statute is stricter than what the CFR sometimes accepts; write, but warn)

| Situation | Statutory basis | Warning text on the checklist |
|---|---|---|
| Applicant already holds a s 13 licence and applies for another s 13 | s 13(3) | "The Act allows one section 13 licence at a time. If you keep your current section 13 firearm this application may be refused. If you are selling it, or re-licensing this same firearm, say so in the motivation." |
| Applicant already holds a s 14 licence | s 14(5) | Same shape. |
| s 15 count would exceed four, or three where a s 13 is held, or a second s 15 handgun | s 15(3) | "You will hold N section 15 licences after this one; the Act allows M. Consider section 16 if you hold dedicated status." |

| s 15 applicant is a member of an accredited association | s 1 definitions | See Part 10.6. |

Dedicated sport shooting is the usual route." |

### 3.3 What the writer must be given (the fact pack)

The writer receives, and may use, only this:

1. Applicant: full names, ID number, residential address (street, suburb, town), occupation and employer name (not the employer's address), citizenship or permanent residence, marital status only if the applicant chose to give it for a s 13 dependants argument.
2. Application: section applied under (13, 14, 15 hunting, 15 sport, 16 hunter, 16 sport, or 24 with the underlying section); source route (dealer, private sale, estate); whether this firearm is already licensed to the applicant under another section (re-licensing).
3. Firearm: type, action, make, model, calibre, serial numbers (barrel, frame or receiver as on the card or dealer form), and for a semi-automatic shotgun the manufactured capacity.
4. Competency: certificate number, date of issue, endorsements (handgun, manual rifle, self-loading rifle or carbine, shotgun). No expiry.
5. Proficiency: unit standards passed, provider, date. Older POSLEC or SASSETA certificates likewise.
6. Battery: every held firearm as a row: make, model, type, calibre, serial, licence section as printed on the card, stated purpose (from the applicant or an endorsement by serial), licence expiry. An empty battery is stated as empty. A count is never sent without the rows.
7. Association (s 15 and s 16): name, SAPS accreditation number(s) as printed on the endorsement, membership number, date joined, dedicated status type and date obtained (s 16 only), last activity report date, endorsement for this serial (yes or no, and the purpose it names).
8. Purpose facts, per section (Part 5): the applicant's own answers about hunting ground, species, ranges, disciplines, matches, routine, movements, incidents, existing security.
9. Storage: residence type, safe type and capacity, how fixed, where, who holds keys, prior DFO inspection if any, transport arrangement.
10. Record: the six SAPS 271 history questions as answered; a Yes comes with station, CAS number, charge and outcome.
11. Crime evidence (s 13 and s 14 only): the SAPS quarterly figures for the home precinct and up to three precincts the applicant said they pass through, each with period, station and release name; each press item as headline, publication, date, SAPS category.
12. Annexure index: letter and exact title for every annexure in the pack.

The writer is never given: phone numbers, spouse ID, postal address, licence numbers of held firearms, a competency expiry, firearm or cartridge research, manufacturer descriptions, discipline rule books, national crime statistics, or any document the applicant did not attach.

## Part 4. Anatomy of the pack and of the motivation

### 4.1 Order of the bound pack

1. SAPS 271 (or SAPS 518(a) for a renewal), pre-filled, unsigned. On a dealer purchase the dealer's SAPS 350(a) stock return is attached behind it; on a private sale the seller's consent letter is attached behind it; on an estate the executor's papers.
2. The motivation (cover page, contents page, body, signature).
3. The request for prior notice and written reasons (the PAJA letter), signed separately.
4. The annexures, each behind a lettered divider, in index order.
5. The applicant's own checklist page ("Take these with you"), which is removed at the counter and never lodged.

### 4.2 The motivation's fixed skeleton

Every motivation, whatever the section, has these parts in this order. Section chapters in Part 5 say what goes into each and which are omitted for a given section. Headings are fixed; they are not varied, paraphrased or numbered differently from one document to the next. A DFO does not compare applicants' letters for plagiarism; they compare the facts to the annexures. Consistent headings make the DFO's job faster, which is the point.

Cover page (Part 7): "Motivation in support of an application for a licence to possess a firearm in terms of section N of the Firearms Control Act 60 of 2000", the purpose in words, the firearm (type, make, model, calibre, serial), the applicant's full names and ID number, the date, and a photograph of the firearm applied for.

Contents page: the numbered headings with page numbers, then the annexure index (letter, title, number of pages, certification flag).

Body headings, in order:

1. Introduction
2. My circumstances (s 13 and s 14 only: where I live and work, my routine and movements, who depends on me)
3. The purpose (the heading is section-specific: "Why I need a firearm for self-defence" / "Why a section 13 firearm will not provide sufficient protection" / "My hunting" / "My sport shooting" / "How I have used this firearm since it was licensed")
4. What I already do and where it stops (s 13 and s 14 only: existing security measures and their limits)
5. The firearm applied for and why it suits the purpose
6. Firearms already licensed to me (a table, then one sentence per firearm on why it cannot do this job; omitted on a first application but then the statement "No firearm is currently licensed to me" appears in the introduction)
7. My competency and training
8. Association membership and dedicated status (s 15: membership only; s 16: membership, dedicated status, endorsement; omitted for s 13 and s 14)
9. Safe storage and transport
10. My record (the six declaration items, addressed only if any is a Yes; otherwise this heading is omitted)
11. Section N applied to my application (the statutory elements quoted, each followed by the fact that meets it)
12. Declaration and request

Then the signature block: "Signed at [place] on [date]", signature, full names, ID number.

### 4.3 Length

Applicant-specific prose, excluding the quoted statute and the table: s 13 900 to 1,400 words; s 14 1,100 to 1,600 words; s 15 1,000 to 1,500 words; s 16 1,200 to 1,800 words; s 24 600 to 1,000 words. These are bands, not quotas. A shorter document built only from the applicant's facts beats a longer one padded to the band. No section other than the purpose section and the firearm section exceeds four paragraphs; the introduction and the declaration are one paragraph each.

### 4.4 The single sentence for SAPS 271 item G.61

The form's motivation box takes one line, generated with the document, in this shape:

"I require this [type] for [purpose in the Act's words]. My full motivation and annexures are attached."

Examples: "I require this handgun for self-defence. My full motivation and annexures are attached." / "I require this rifle for dedicated hunting. My full motivation and annexures are attached." No "Regards", no signature in the box.

## Part 5. Section chapters

Each chapter gives the writer: the test, what the DFO is looking for, the section-by-section brief with word budgets, the wording that works, the wording that fails, and the annexures and checklist items specific to the section. The writer receives only the chapter that applies, together with Parts 1, 6 and 8.

### 5.1 Section 13: self-defence (handgun or manual shotgun)

The test. Two limbs, both factual: (a) the applicant needs a firearm for self-defence; (b) that need cannot reasonably be satisfied by other means. The Registrar's decision is discretionary. The second limb is the one most motivations never make: a document that lists security measures without saying where each one stops has asked for a firearm as a first resort.

What the DFO is looking for. The SAPS 271 gives it away: where the applicant lives and works, distances, whether the area is crime-rated, whether the work or residence is high-risk, how many firearms the applicant already has, and whether the safe is right. The DFO also reads the battery against the request: an applicant with a s 16 handgun asking for a s 13 handgun must explain in one sentence why the s 16 licence does not cover self-defence (it is issued for a different purpose) and, if the firearm is the same one, that it is being re-licensed.

Section briefs.

1. Introduction (one paragraph, 60 to 100 words). Who I am (full names, ID number), where I live (suburb and town), what I do, and the sentence that frames the document: "I apply under section 13 of the Firearms Control Act 60 of 2000 for a licence to possess a [make] [model] [calibre] [handgun/shotgun], serial [serial], for self-defence." If nothing is licensed to the applicant: "No firearm is currently licensed to me." If the firearm is already held under another section: "This firearm is currently licensed to me under section 16 for [purpose]; I apply to license it under section 13 because I need it for self-defence, which the section 16 licence does not cover."

2. My circumstances (two to three paragraphs, 200 to 350 words). The home: type of dwelling, who lives there, who depends on the applicant, the character of the street at night. The work: occupation, where, hours, travel, cash or equipment carried, sites visited, overnight stays, alone or not. Movements: the routes and times that recur (the early commute, the late return, the farm road, the fishing spot, the hiking trail), stated as the applicant's own routine, with no hunting or sport words. Facts, not adjectives.

3. Why I need a firearm for self-defence (two to four paragraphs, 250 to 400 words). This is the heart of the s 13(2)(a) argument and it is built from three things only: the precinct figures for the home station and the stations the applicant passes through, quoted exactly with the category, the number, the period and the release name ("Kraaifontein recorded 95 burglaries at residential premises and 16 robberies at residential premises in April to June 2026, SAPS quarterly crime statistics, first quarter 2026/27"); specific incidents the applicant reported (with CAS number, or the press item's publication and date) tied to a place on the applicant's routine; and the honest sentence about response: "A response takes minutes; an attack takes seconds." No national totals, no atmosphere, no adjectives about crime, no translated headline words (the SAPS category is "common robbery" or "robbery at residential premises", not "grip attacks"). Never claim an incident the applicant did not report. The paragraph ends by connecting the figures to the routine: the categories that matter are the ones that happen where and when the applicant is.

4. What I already do and where it stops (one to two paragraphs, 120 to 220 words). Each measure in place (wall, electric fence, alarm, armed response, burglar bars, gates, dogs, lighting, a security estate) with one clause on what it protects and one on where it stops: the alarm sounds after entry; armed response is a call and a drive away; the gate is the point at which the applicant is exposed in the vehicle; none of them travel with the applicant on the road. Never disparage the police or the armed-response company. The closing sentence makes the s 13(2)(b) point in the applicant's own terms: the alternative to a firearm is to stop doing the things that expose them (to stop working the late shifts, to stop driving those roads), and that is not reasonable because [income, family, the nature of the work].

5. The firearm applied for and why it suits the purpose (one paragraph, at most 60 words, four facts). Type and make; calibre; why a handgun rather than a rifle or shotgun (it can be carried on the person and kept close at home); one sentence that the calibre is a common service calibre whose ammunition is readily available for practice. Nothing else: no action type, no barrel length, no capacity, no trigger, no frame material, no ballistics, no comparison with other models, no manufacturer history. For a manual shotgun under s 13: type, gauge, that it is manually operated, and that it is kept for the home, not carried.

6. Firearms already licensed to me (table plus 30 to 60 words per firearm). The table (make and model, type, calibre, serial, section, licensed purpose, expiry) is generated from the licence cards. Then one sentence per firearm, built only from what the card and the stated use prove: "The [make] [calibre] rifle is licensed under section 16 for hunting; a rifle cannot be carried on the person and a section 16 licence is not issued for self-defence." Where no purpose is stated: "The [make] [calibre] is licensed under section 16." and stop. Never invent a role, never write "backup" or "secondary", never describe a held firearm with sport or hunting adjectives it was not given.

7. My competency and training (one paragraph, 60 to 120 words). "I hold competency certificate number [number], issued on [date], endorsed for handguns." Then the unit standards passed and the provider, and any refresher or practice the applicant actually stated. No expiry date. No reloading.

9. Safe storage and transport (one paragraph, 100 to 180 words). Part 6.6.

10. My record (only if any SAPS 271 G.62 to G.67 answer is Yes; one short paragraph). "In [year] a case was opened at [station] under CAS [number] for [charge]; the outcome was [outcome]." Nothing else. If every answer is No, the heading is omitted and nothing is said.

11. Section 13 applied to my application (the quoted elements 13(1), 13(2)(a), 13(2)(b), 13(3), each followed by one or two sentences pointing back to the facts above). Part 8.

12. Declaration and request (one paragraph, 60 to 100 words). Part 8.5.

Wording that works (from approved s 13 letters):
- "At best the response of the SA Police will be in minutes but a life might be taken or lost within seconds during an attack."
- "If I cannot protect my life and that of my family adequately and within reason, I will have to be at home continuously. That I cannot reasonably do, as it will have a serious impact on my income."
- "I am aware that should the need ever arise for me to draw my firearm to protect my life or that of my loved ones, it must be a last resort."
- "The CZ is a well-known firearm, but it is too bulky and heavy to carry as a self-protection firearm and I cannot conceal it under my clothes as the Act requires."

Wording that fails:
- "peace of mind", "in case", "you never know", "the crime situation in South Africa" as a reason.
- "I have no criminal record", "I am a law-abiding citizen", "I am a healthy, stable and meticulous person" (unevidenced, and SAPS checks the first itself).
- "I am aware of my constitutional right to own a firearm" (there is none).
- "high capacity", "stopping power", "terminal ballistics", "effective operational range", "platform", "engage", "tactical", "concealed carry" as a product feature.
- Any mention of hunting, sport, competition, reloading or collecting, except in the one sentence describing a held firearm's licence.
- "urgently", "the urgency of approval".
- The employer's street address, the spouse's ID number, marital status stated for its own sake.

Annexures specific to s 13: the precinct figures table and press screenshots (Part 9.2 "Crime evidence"); an incident report or CAS printout where the applicant has one; a security or armed-response contract where the applicant chose to attach it. No association papers. No hunting or sport evidence.

Checklist notes specific to s 13: the DFO may complete section K of the SAPS 271 by asking the applicant the same questions; the applicant should be able to answer the distance to the nearest police station and neighbours from memory.

### 5.2 Section 14: restricted firearm for self-defence (semi-automatic rifle or shotgun)

The test. Everything in s 13(2), plus two more things the applicant must show: that a s 13(1) firearm (a handgun or manual shotgun) "will not provide sufficient protection", and "reasonable information to motivate the need for a restricted firearm". A s 14 licence lasts two years and only one may be held. It is the hardest of the five to obtain and the motivation must be the most concrete.

What the DFO is looking for. SAPS 271 section K is completed for s 14 only: urban, rural, farm or smallholding; distance to the nearest neighbours and to the nearest police station; whether the area is crime-rated; whether the residence or work is dangerous or high-risk; how many firearms the applicant holds. The motivation must state every one of those facts so the DFO can transcribe them.

Section briefs. Same skeleton as s 13 with these changes.

2. My circumstances (three to four paragraphs, 300 to 450 words). Adds: the exact nature of the premises (farm, smallholding, remote business, size, boundaries, approach roads), distances in kilometres to the nearest neighbour and police station, response times the applicant has actually experienced, the number of people on the premises and their ages, any livestock or business assets that make the premises a target.

3. Why a section 13 firearm will not provide sufficient protection (three to four paragraphs, 300 to 450 words). This heading replaces the s 13 purpose heading and carries both limbs of s 14(4). First the threat, argued exactly as for s 13 (precinct figures for the rural station, reported incidents, the pattern of farm attacks or business robberies in that precinct where the applicant has evidence). Then why a handgun or manual shotgun is not sufficient: the number of attackers in the incidents reported for that area; the distances at which a threat appears on open ground; the time to reload a manual action when alone; the applicant's physical circumstances where relevant; that the firearm stays on the premises and is not carried on the person. Every point must be a fact the applicant gave; the writer does not supply tactical theory. State the manufactured capacity plainly and without emphasis.

4. What I already do and where it stops. As for s 13, with more weight: fencing, dogs, lighting, radio or WhatsApp farm-watch network, armed response distance, the existing s 13 firearm if held and why it is insufficient.

5. The firearm applied for. Two paragraphs at most, 120 words: type, make, model, calibre, that it is semi-automatic and cannot readily be converted to fully automatic (s 14(1)(a)), manufactured capacity, and one sentence on why that type answers the deficiency described above. No ballistics, no product copy, no "battle-proven".

11. Section 14 applied to my application: elements 14(1)(a), 14(4) first limb, 14(4) second limb, 14(5).

Wording that fails: anything military ("engage", "suppress", "firepower", "tactical", "rate of fire", "4 shots a second"), anything about hunting or sport, any suggestion the firearm will be carried in public.

Annexures specific to s 14: as for s 13, plus a sketch or map of the premises if the applicant supplied one, and proof of the premises (title deed, lease, or the proof-of-address document) showing the rural or high-risk location.

Checklist notes: the DFO will inspect the premises; the applicant should expect section K to be completed on site or at the counter.

### 5.3 Section 15: occasional hunting or occasional sports-shooting

The test. Only that the applicant is in fact an occasional hunter or occasional sports person (participates "from time to time") and that the firearm is of a permitted type and the count stays within s 15(3). There is no need test. The motivation is evidentiary: it shows participation, access to ground or ranges, and why this type and calibre fit that participation.

Headings 9 (Safe storage and transport), 10 (My record, only where a declaration answer is Yes) and 12 (Declaration and request) follow the s 13 briefs in Part 5.1; headings 2 and 4 are omitted.

Two variants: s 15 hunting and s 15 sport. The generator picks one from the applicant's answer; the document never mixes both unless the applicant genuinely does both, and then hunting leads.

What the DFO is looking for. That the count on SAPS 271 G.2 plus this application is within s 15(3); that the competency matches; that the applicant can show some real hunting or shooting (a farm invitation, a hunting permit, past hunts, club shoot results); and that the firearm is not a semi-automatic rifle or shotgun.

Section briefs.

1. Introduction (60 to 100 words). Who, where, occupation; "I apply under section 15 ... for a licence to possess a [firearm] for occasional [hunting / sports-shooting]." The count sentence, stated plainly: "I currently hold [N] licences issued under section 15 [and one under section 13]; after this application I will hold [N+1], within the limit in section 15(3)." First application: "No firearm is currently licensed to me."

3. My hunting (s 15 hunting; two to four paragraphs, 250 to 400 words). Where the applicant hunts (province, district, the farms by name where an invitation or past hunt exists), how often and in which season, what species, at what ranges and in what terrain (bushveld, open plains, mountain), with whom, and how the meat is used. Past hunts with year and species where the applicant gave them. The farm letter or invitation is cited here. Hunting ethics in one sentence at most, in the applicant's own words, never an essay.

3. My sport shooting (s 15 sport; two to four paragraphs, 250 to 400 words). Where the applicant shoots (the range or club by name), how often, in what format (club shoots, informal target days, association open days), with what results if any, and what the applicant intends to enter. Club membership is cited where it exists. No "dedicated", no dedicated-status language, no association exercise rule books.

5. The firearm applied for and why it suits the purpose (two to three paragraphs, 150 to 250 words). Part 6.2 (hunting) or 6.3 (sport). The link is terrain, species and range for hunting; the format and distance for sport. Calibre is justified in two or three sentences of the applicant's reasoning, not a ballistics table.

6. Firearms already licensed to me. Table plus one sentence per firearm on the gap: "My [make] .22 LR is a rimfire for small game and cannot ethically take the medium game I have been invited to hunt." Never disparage the held firearm; state its role and the gap.

7. My competency and training. Competency number, date, endorsement; unit standards; any hunting or shooting course the applicant completed.

8. Association membership (one paragraph, only where the applicant is a member). "I am a member of [association], membership number [number], since [date]." Nothing about dedicated status. If the association endorsed this firearm for occasional hunting or sport, one sentence and the annexure citation.

11. Section 15 applied to my application: 15(1) (type and action), 15(2) (occasional participation, pointing to the hunting or sport section), 15(3) (the count).

Wording that fails: "dedicated" in any sentence about the applicant; self-defence or protection words; second-person how-to text ("aim your shot up the foreleg"); Wikipedia calibre paragraphs; "the .30-06 is the best all-round cartridge"; pasted outfitter species descriptions; invented hunt counts ("I hunt approximately 20 times a year").

Annexures specific to s 15: farm letter or hunting invitation; hunting permits or past permits; hunt photographs (only where the applicant supplied them); club membership or shoot results; association membership certificate; the association's s 15 endorsement if issued.

### 5.4 Section 16: dedicated hunter

The test. Two elements: the applicant is a dedicated hunter (actively participates and is a member of an accredited hunting association), and the application is accompanied by the chairperson's sworn statement or solemn declaration of registered membership. No numeric cap and no statutory need test, but the licence is discretionary, so the document must show why this firearm serves the dedicated activity and how it fits the battery.

What the DFO is looking for. SAPS 271 G.54 to G.60 completed (association, SAPS accreditation (FAR) number, membership number, date joined, expiry); the chairperson's letter; the dedicated status certificate; that the applicant's activity reporting to the association is current (associations will not endorse otherwise); the endorsement naming this serial; the competency matching the action (a semi-automatic rifle needs the self-loading endorsement); and a battery argument that shows the new firearm fills a gap rather than duplicates.

Section briefs. Headings 9, 10 and 12 follow the s 13 briefs in Part 5.1; headings 2 and 4 are omitted.

1. Introduction (60 to 100 words). Who, where, occupation; "I am a dedicated hunter, registered with [association] (SAPS accreditation number [number]), membership number [number], dedicated status granted on [date]. I apply under section 16 ... for a licence to possess a [firearm] for dedicated hunting."

3. My hunting (three to four paragraphs, 300 to 450 words). The applicant's hunting in detail: years hunting, the areas and farms, the seasons, the species taken in recent years with year, the ranges and terrain, the hunting methods (walk and stalk, from a hide, at night for vermin where lawful), the use of the meat, the role of the association's activities (hunting days, culling operations, association exercises the applicant shot), and the activity report the applicant last submitted. Past hunts are stated as the applicant gave them; nothing is rounded up.

5. The firearm applied for and why it suits the purpose (three to four paragraphs, 200 to 350 words). Part 6.2. For a semi-automatic rifle: the lawful use it is for (vermin control or culling on land the applicant owns or has standing access to, or the association's semi-automatic exercises where hunting-side endorsement was refused), the association's endorsement, and never any defensive framing. For a semi-automatic shotgun: the manufactured capacity stated, with the s 16(1)(c) five-shot point made plainly where it complies.

6. Firearms already licensed to me. The table, then the gap sentence per firearm, using calibre class, range, species and terrain: "My 6.5 Creedmoor bolt-action is my open-plains rifle for springbok and blesbok to 300 metres; it is too light for the buffalo-country bushveld hunting on [farm] where shots are under 80 metres and the game is eland and kudu, which is what the .45-70 lever action is for." Where two held firearms cover the same class, say so and give the honest reason for another (a specific hunt, a specific species, a rifle set up for a specific method), or the application is weak and the writer says so to the generator (Part 11).

7. My competency and training. Competency number, date, endorsements; unit standards; the association's dedicated hunter course by name and date; any other hunting qualification.

8. Association membership and dedicated status (one to two paragraphs, 100 to 180 words). Association, accreditation number, membership number, date joined, dedicated status type and date, last activity report, endorsement for this serial and what it says, and the chairperson's sworn statement cited by annexure letter. Quote nothing from the association's marketing.

11. Section 16 applied to my application: 16(1) (type; for a semi-auto shotgun the capacity), 16(2) first part (dedicated hunter: membership plus active participation, pointing to sections 3 and 8), 16(2) second part (the sworn statement, cited).

Wording that fails: "dedicated" used to mean "used for"; ballistics pasted from a reloading manual; species pages copied from an outfitter; "the previous Act allowed me to borrow"; "the immortal .45-70"; any self-defence, backup, protection or home-defence word; "urgent".

Annexures specific to s 16 hunter: association membership certificate; dedicated hunter status certificate; the chairperson's sworn statement or letter of good standing (the s 16(2) document); the association's endorsement for this serial; the latest activity report or verified activity log; farm letters and hunting permits; hunt photographs where supplied; proof of land ownership where a semi-automatic firearm is applied for on the landowner route.

### 5.5 Section 16: dedicated sports shooter

The test. As for the dedicated hunter, with an accredited sports-shooting organisation. The discretionary argument is discipline-based: a named discipline or exercise, the format and distance, the equipment that discipline calls for, and the applicant's participation.

What the DFO is looking for. G.54 to G.60; the sworn statement; dedicated status; current activity reporting; the endorsement naming the serial and the discipline; competency matching the action; that the firearm is credible for the named discipline (a 6.35 mm pocket pistol is not an IPSC firearm; a fixed-choke tactical shotgun is not a clay gun); and the battery gap.

Section briefs. Headings 9, 10 and 12 follow the s 13 briefs in Part 5.1; headings 2 and 4 are omitted.

1. Introduction. "I am a dedicated sports shooter, registered with [organisation] (SAPS accreditation number [number]), membership number [number], dedicated status granted on [date]. I apply under section 16 ... for dedicated sports-shooting."

3. My sport shooting (three to four paragraphs, 300 to 450 words). The disciplines and exercises the applicant actually shoots (by the organisation's own names), where (ranges and clubs by name), how often (as stated), the club memberships, results and classifications where the applicant has them, the exercises the applicant intends to enter with this firearm, and the last activity report. Where the organisation's exercise has a printed rule the applicant supplied (calibre class, action class, distance), the rule is stated in one sentence and the rule sheet is annexed; otherwise no rule is claimed.

5. The firearm applied for and why it suits the purpose (three to four paragraphs, 200 to 350 words). Part 6.3. The discipline drives the description: a self-loading rifle for the organisation's semi-automatic rifle exercise or 3-gun; a 9 mm pistol for practical pistol in the production division; a semi-automatic shotgun (five-shot) for clays and steel; a long-range bolt action for 600 to 1,000 metre falling plates. The applicant's own reasoning connects the firearm to the format ("the exercise is shot standing at 10 metres in rapid strings; a full-size 9 mm with a longer sight radius and lower recoil than my compact carry pistol is what the format rewards"). One paragraph on how it fits the battery.

6. Firearms already licensed to me. Table plus gap sentences by discipline, division, action and range: "My CZ 9 mm is licensed under section 13 for self-defence; it is a compact carry pistol and a section 13 licence is not issued for sport." "My .308 bolt action is my hunting rifle and is not competitive beyond 350 metres on the long-range plates, which is what the 6.5 Creedmoor is for."

7. My competency and training. Number, date, endorsements, unit standards, the organisation's dedicated sports shooter course, club courses (range officer, safety) where completed.

8. Association membership and dedicated status. As for the hunter, naming the sports-shooting organisation and the club.

11. Section 16 applied to my application: as for the hunter, pointing at the sport section.

Wording that fails: "race gun", "the king's class", "high-volume", "split times", "power factor" (unless quoting a supplied rule sheet), "competitively", divisions that do not exist in South Africa (Carry Optics, Limited 10, Stock Service Pistol), a discipline the firearm cannot enter, "concealed carry" or "home defence" in any sentence about the firearm applied for, manufacturer copy about law-enforcement or home-defence use or rate of fire, sport history essays, lists of ranges in South Africa.

Annexures specific to s 16 sport: as for the hunter, with shoot results, classification or scores report, club membership, competition certificates, and the organisation's exercise rule sheet for the firearm class where the applicant supplied it.

### 5.6 Section 24: renewal

The test. The applicant "has continued to comply with the requirements for the licence in terms of this Act" (s 24(3)). That is the original section's test applied to today: for s 13, the need still exists and cannot reasonably be met otherwise; for s 14, a s 13 firearm is still insufficient; for s 15, the applicant still hunts or shoots from time to time; for s 16, the applicant is still a dedicated hunter or sports person in good standing with the association. Plus continued compliance with everything else: competency valid (renewed with the licence under s 10A), safe custody, no unfitness.

Form and timing. SAPS 518(a), lodged at least 90 days before expiry at the DFO of residence. If lodged in time the licence remains valid until decided (s 24(4)); the acknowledgement of receipt (SAPS 523) proves it (reg 13(13)). The competency renewal (SAPS 517(g) in current practice) is lodged with it where the competency is tied to this licence, on a sworn declaration of continued s 9(2) compliance; no re-test (s 10A(7)).

The document is short: 600 to 1,000 words. It does not re-argue the original application from scratch; it shows continuity and any change.

Section briefs.

1. Introduction (60 to 100 words). "I apply under section 24 for the renewal of licence number [number], issued under section [N] on [date] and expiring on [date], for my [firearm]. I have held and used this firearm under that licence since [year]."

3. How I have used this firearm since it was licensed (two to three paragraphs, 200 to 350 words). For s 13 or s 14: the circumstances that gave rise to the need and whether they have changed; incidents since; the current precinct figures for the home station (one paragraph, same rules as s 13); the security measures still in place. For s 15: hunts or shoots since licensing, with years and places as given. For s 16: the association, continued dedicated status, activity reports submitted each year, hunts or matches since licensing, the endorsement or letter of good standing for the renewal.

5. The firearm. One short paragraph confirming the firearm is unchanged (make, model, calibre, serial) and in the applicant's possession.

6. Firearms already licensed to me. The table; no gap argument (a firearm does not duplicate itself). One sentence if the battery has changed since the original licence.

7. My competency and training. Number and endorsements; the competency renewal lodged with this application where applicable.

8. Association membership and dedicated status (s 16 renewals only).

9. Safe storage and transport. Current safe, unchanged or changed; last DFO inspection.

10. My record. Only if any declaration answer is Yes.

11. Section 24 applied to my application: s 24(1) (lodged at least 90 days before expiry, with the date), s 24(3) (continued compliance, pointing to the sections above and to the original section's elements in one sentence each).

12. Declaration and request: request the renewal of the licence, naming the licence number, section, firearm and purpose.

Wording that fails: padding a renewal into a new application; arguing why the firearm does not duplicate itself; "nothing has changed" without the facts that show it.

Annexures specific to s 24: the current licence (front and back); the competency certificate and, where lodged, the competency renewal form; for s 16 the current letter of good standing, dedicated certificate and latest activity report; for s 13 and s 14 the current precinct figures; safe photographs; proof of address; certified ID.

## Part 6. Arguing the firearm, the battery and the storage

### 6.1 The general rule

Purpose before firearm. The reader must already know what the applicant does and needs before the firearm is described, and the firearm is described only in the terms that connect it to that purpose. Every sentence about the firearm answers "what does this feature do for the stated purpose", never "what is this firearm like".

The writer knows nothing about firearms that the applicant did not say. A make, model, calibre, action, capacity, barrel length or feature appears only if it is in the fact pack. Where the applicant said nothing technical, the argument is made from the purpose and the type alone and the paragraph is shorter.

### 6.2 Hunting firearms (s 15 hunting, s 16 hunter)

The chain of reasoning, in the applicant's words, is: the ground (terrain, cover, typical shot distance), the game (species and size), the method (walk and stalk, hide, night vermin), then the firearm that answers those three (action, calibre class, weight, handling), then how it fits beside what is already held.

Acceptable technical content: the calibre's class in plain words as the applicant gave it (a light rimfire for small game; a medium centre-fire for plains game to 300 metres; a heavy short-range cartridge for thick bush), the action and why it suits the method (a fast-cycling lever action in thick bush; a bolt action for a steady first shot at range), the weight and length if the applicant walks far. Not acceptable: velocity and energy tables, bullet weights and construction, sectional density, comparative charts, manufacturer or cartridge history, quotations from reloading manuals or magazines, the phrase "one-shot kill".

The ethical sentence, once, if the applicant gave it: the applicant chooses the calibre that will take the animal cleanly at the distances hunted, and does not hunt game the firearm is too light for.

### 6.3 Sport firearms (s 15 sport, s 16 sports shooter)

The chain: the discipline or exercise by the organisation's name, its format (distance, time, strings, targets, positions), the equipment class or division it is shot in, then the firearm feature that the format rewards, then the battery gap.

Acceptable: the exercise's own printed rule where the applicant supplied it (calibre floor, action class, distance), the division the applicant will enter, the features named in the applicant's own reasoning (sight radius, trigger, recoil, weight, magazine as the division allows). Not acceptable: "power factor" and "split times" unless quoting a supplied rule, "competitively", "high-volume", "platform", divisions that are not shot in South Africa, a discipline the firearm is not credible for, product copy about reliability or ergonomics, sport history.

### 6.4 Self-defence firearms (s 13, s 14)

s 13: the sixty-word paragraph in Part 5.1. s 14: the two paragraphs in Part 5.2. Nothing more. The argument for a self-defence firearm is the circumstances, not the firearm.

### 6.5 The battery

The table is generated from the licence cards and stated uses; the writer never composes it. Columns: make and model; type; calibre; serial; section (as printed on the card); licensed purpose (stated or blank); expiry. Where nothing is licensed the table is replaced by the sentence "No firearm is currently licensed to me" in the introduction and the heading is omitted.

After the table, one sentence per held firearm, with three permitted arguments and no others:

1. Section: "a section 16 licence is issued for hunting or sport, not for self-defence" (or the reverse). A licence is issued under a section for a purpose and that licence does not cover this application's purpose. The sentence says the licence does not cover the purpose; it does not say the firearm may never be used for it.
2. Type or action: a rifle cannot be carried on the person; a manual action cannot shoot the semi-automatic exercise; a rimfire cannot take medium game; a compact pistol gives away sight radius in a precision event.
3. Range, species, terrain or discipline: from the applicant's stated use of that firearm.

Where the held firearm has no stated purpose, the sentence names it and its section and stops. Where a held firearm plainly does the same job as the one applied for and the applicant gave no distinguishing reason, the writer does not manufacture one; it writes the honest sentence ("My [make] [calibre] would serve the same hunting; I apply for this rifle because [the applicant's stated reason]") and the generator flags the application as weak to the applicant before release (Part 11).

Words never used about a held firearm: backup, secondary, spare, practice gun (unless the applicant said so and the section allows it), "not my match gun", or any role that was not supplied.

### 6.6 Safe storage and transport

One paragraph, 100 to 180 words, from the applicant's facts: the residence type; the safe (type, capacity, make if given), how it is fixed (bolted through the wall or floor with the bolts visible in the photograph), where it stands (a locked room or garage giving a second locked barrier), who holds the keys or combination ("I am the only key holder"), that ammunition is stored separately or in the safe's ammunition compartment as the applicant said; that the safe conforms to SABS 953-1 / 953-2 as required by regulation 86 (the applicant's statement, backed by the safe invoice or the DFO's previous inspection where those exist); a previous DFO inspection with date if any; the invitation to inspect; and one sentence on transport (unloaded, in a locked case, ammunition separate, direct to the range or the farm). Never describe the alarm zones, beam positions or wall heights in detail; that is a map for a burglar.

The three photographs are cited once: "Photographs of the safe closed, half open with the key in the door, and of the bolts fixing it to the wall are at Annexure [letter]."

## Part 7. Layout and visual structure

The lodged motivation is read on paper by a police official with a pile of them. It must look like the professionally prepared packs a DFO already knows (the Engala and association-template packs), not like a printout of a chat. These rules are house rules built from those packs.

### 7.1 Page and type

- A4 portrait, 25 mm margins, single column, left-aligned (ragged right, never justified), 11 pt serif body with 1.3 line spacing, 14 pt bold headings, 10 pt footer. Black on white. No colour except in photographs and tables' light rules.
- Running footer on every body page: applicant's full names, ID number, "Motivation: [make] [model] [calibre], serial [serial], section N", and "Page n of N". A signature line ("Initial: ______") in the footer of every page of the motivation so the applicant initials each page, as the Engala packs do.
- Running header: none on the cover; on body pages the short title "Motivation for a section N licence".
- Headings printed as headings: numbered, on their own line, bold, with a blank line before. Never inline, never with a trailing colon, never varied in wording between documents.
- Paragraphs separated by a blank line, no first-line indent, no bullets or numbered lists inside the prose (lists belong in tables and the annexure index).
- Widows and orphans controlled; a heading never sits at the foot of a page; a table never splits across pages if it fits on one.
- No watermark of any kind on the final. A draft watermark, if used, is an image, never text glyphs.

### 7.2 Cover page

Centred, in this order: the addressee ("The Registrar of Firearms, Central Firearms Register, South African Police Service, Pretoria, through the Designated Firearms Officer, [station]"); the title in caps ("MOTIVATION IN SUPPORT OF AN APPLICATION FOR A LICENCE TO POSSESS A FIREARM IN TERMS OF SECTION 16 OF THE FIREARMS CONTROL ACT 60 OF 2000"); the purpose line ("Dedicated hunting"); a particulars table (Applicant, Identity number, Firearm type and action, Make, Model, Calibre, Serial number(s), Section applied under, Date); a photograph of the firearm applied for (the dealer's or seller's photograph, or the applicant's), 90 mm wide, captioned with make, model, calibre and serial. Nothing else. No logo, no service name.

### 7.3 Contents page

Two blocks. "Contents": the twelve body headings that appear, with page numbers. "Annexures": letter, title, number of pages, and the certification flag ("Certified copy" for the ID, competency and proficiency; blank for the rest). The page numbers must be the numbers the pages end up with after the annexures are merged; the index is generated last.

### 7.4 Tables in the body

Three tables only: the battery table (Part 6.5), the competency table (certificate number, date of issue, endorsements, unit standards, provider) and, for s 13 and s 14, the precinct figures table (station, category, count, period, release). Light horizontal rules, header row bold, 10 pt, never shaded, never coloured.

### 7.5 The statutory block

The quoted subsections are set in a lightly indented block in the same serif at 10.5 pt, each element on its own line with its number, and the applicant's sentence under each element in normal body type. The block is generated by the renderer from Part 2; the writer supplies only the sentences.

### 7.6 Signature block

Directly after the declaration, never on a page of its own: "Signed at ______________ on ____ / ____ / 20____", a signature line, the full names and ID number printed beneath. The PAJA letter has its own signature block.

### 7.7 Annexure dividers and copies

Each annexure begins on a divider page: the letter in 48 pt, the title in 14 pt, "(n pages)" and, for a lettered group of several copies, the sub-captions ("Annexure F (1 of 4): licence, CZ 9 mm Parabellum, serial ..."). Copies are printed one per page, scaled to fit with at least 30 mm clear at the foot for the certification stamp on those that carry a certification block (Part 9.4). Photographs are printed at least 120 mm wide with a caption ("Safe, door closed, key removed"). The precinct table and press screenshots follow the same one-item-per-page rule.

### 7.8 What the examples do that this layout keeps

From the professional packs: the title page with the full statutory title; the contents and annexure list; the running footer with name, ID and initial line; the lettered annexure list (placed on the contents page rather than at the end of the body); the one-page PAJA letter; the photograph of the firearm. From the association templates: the numbered heading spine that mirrors the SAPS 271's own sections, the battery table, the safe statement with three photographs. What is left out: pasted articles, sport history, range lists, species pages, ballistics charts, hyperlinks, marketing images.

## Part 8. Writing rules for the model

### 8.1 Voice and register

Written by the applicant, to the Registrar, in plain formal English, as an adult explaining their circumstances to an official who will check every sentence. Short sentences, one idea each. Concrete nouns and numbers where the facts have them. No adjectives doing the work of facts. No rhetorical questions. No exclamation marks. No emotional appeals, no fear language, no gratitude in advance, no threats of appeal.

The document sounds like a person, not a template, because the facts are the applicant's own and are placed where the reader needs them; not because the headings or sentence rhythms are randomised. Two applicants with the same section will have the same headings and different content.

### 8.2 Sentence patterns that read as natural

- Fact, then consequence: "My shift ends at 22:00 and I drive home alone on the R101; the last 4 km has no street lighting."
- Measure, then limit: "The armed-response contract covers the house; it does not cover the gate, where I sit in the vehicle waiting for it to open."
- Held firearm, then gap: "My 6.5 Creedmoor is my open-plains rifle to 300 metres; on [farm] the bush shots are under 80 metres at eland, which it is too light for."
- Statute element, then fact: "Section 15(2) requires that I am an occasional hunter. I have hunted on [farm] in 2024 and 2025 and hold an invitation for the 2026 season (Annexure [letter])."
- Honest limitation stated plainly: "I have no competition record yet; I have shot four club days this year and intend to enter the association's league next season."

### 8.3 Phrases and words that are banned

Slop and filler: "in today's day and age", "it is important to note", "it goes without saying", "as mentioned above", "furthermore", "moreover", "in conclusion", "I would like to take this opportunity", "kindly", "humbly request", "your esteemed office", "at the end of the day", "peace of mind", "ensure the safety", "demonstrating my commitment", "showcases", "leverage", "utilise" (write "use"), "in order to" (write "to"), "a wide range of", "the realities of", "these realities expose me", "compliance with all regulatory requirements", "responsible firearm owner" as a self-description.

Product-page and gun-magazine words: platform, tactical, terminal ballistics, stopping power, high capacity, magazine capacity, muzzle energy, muzzle velocity, foot-pounds, grain (as a bullet weight), expansion, penetration, ergonomic, striker-fired, polymer frame, short-recoil, tilting barrel, battle-proven, combat-proven, proven track record, state-of-the-art, cutting-edge, reliability under, engage targets, dynamic, high-volume, split times, power factor (unless quoting a supplied rule), race gun, immortal, legendary, the best all-round, a wallop, funky, out of this world.

Outcome and pressure words: urgent, urgently, should succeed, likely to be approved, meets the threshold, must grant, favourable consideration, successful application, entitled to a licence, my right to own.

Wrong-section words (Part 1, rule 6): in a s 13 or s 14 document no hunt, hunting, quarry, game, species, sport, competition, discipline, match, club shoot, reloading, handload, primer, propellant, collect; in a s 15 or s 16 document no self-defence, protection, protect, carry, concealed, backup, home defence, defensive, attack, threat.

Americanisms: caliber, defense, offense, meters, utilize, authorize, organize, recognize, specialize, maximize, minimize, program (as an activity).

Non-South-African divisions and terms: Carry Optics, Limited 10, Stock Service Pistol, USPSA, "conceal carry permit".

Claims the writer cannot make: "I have no criminal record"; "SAPS has inspected my safe" unless the applicant gave the date; any competency expiry date; any distance in metres, count of hunts, number of matches, or year that the applicant did not supply; any exercise rule not supplied; any species, farm, range or club not supplied; "approximately" or "about" in front of a figure the applicant did not give.

### 8.4 The statutory section

The renderer prints the quoted elements from Part 2. The writer writes one or two sentences under each element, in this shape: "[Element in plain words]. [The fact from the sections above that meets it], see [heading or annexure]." The writer never paraphrases the statute in its own words elsewhere in the document, never quotes any other section, never quotes s 13(4), 14(6), 15(4) or 16(3), and never writes "I comply with section N" as a bare assertion.

### 8.5 The declaration and request

One paragraph, in this order, in the applicant's words: that the contents are true and correct to the applicant's knowledge; that the applicant understands that a false statement is an offence under section 120(9)(f); that the applicant will store and use the firearm as the Act requires and will notify the Registrar of any change of address or circumstances while the application is pending; and the request itself: "I respectfully request that a licence be issued to me under section N of the Firearms Control Act 60 of 2000 to possess the [make] [model] [calibre] [type], serial [serial], for [purpose]." No summary of the document, no thanks, no "I trust".

Model wording, drawn from approved letters: "I declare that the contents of this motivation are true and correct to the best of my knowledge. I understand that it is an offence in terms of section 120(9)(f) of the Firearms Control Act 60 of 2000 to supply false information in an application. I have no objection to confirming the contents under oath. I will continue to comply with the Act and the Regulations, and I will inform the Registrar of any change in my circumstances while this application is pending. I respectfully request that a licence be issued to me under section 16 of the Act to possess the Marlin 1895 .45-70 Government lever-action rifle, serial MR90189D, for dedicated hunting."

### 8.6 Self-check the writer runs before returning the text

The writer returns the body only, with the fixed headings on their own lines, no markdown, no bullets, and confirms internally that: the full names, ID number, make, model, calibre and serial appear verbatim; every annexure cited exists in the supplied index; no held firearm carries a section or role not supplied; no banned word appears; no number appears that was not supplied; the section discipline holds; the word band is met or the document is shorter because facts were fewer; the last paragraph is the request.

Page one: a table per precinct cited (home station and up to three stations on the applicant's stated routes): station, category (SAPS wording), count, period, and the release name and date of the SAPS quarterly crime statistics from which the figures come. Following pages: one press item per page as a screenshot of the published page showing the masthead, headline, date and standfirst, with the URL and date accessed printed beneath. ### 9.4 Certification blocks (house rule, chosen 9 September 2026)

Three annexures carry a certification block printed in the clear 30 mm at the foot of each copy: the ID (A), the competency certificate (B) and the proficiency statement (C). The index marks A "Certified copy required (regulation 13(4)(b))" and B and C "Certified copy (usually required by the DFO)". All other copies are plain and the applicant shows the originals at the counter. A police station certifies copies free of charge, as do most Commissioners of Oaths (practice); the copy should be certified within three months of lodging.

### 9.5 SAPS 271 (and 518(a)) alignment

The form is pre-filled from the same data as the motivation and is signed only in front of the DFO. What must agree with the motivation: D (section and validity; the printed validity column on the form is out of date only for s 20 and is correct for ss 13 to 16); E (type, action, calibre, make, model, every serial as printed on the source document, including "NONE" where the card prints it); F (dealer: left for the dealer's SAPS 350(a) details; private sale: the seller completes F.1.2 type A, F.3 to F.15 and F.79 to F.87; estate: type E); G.1 (competency type ticks, number, issue date, expiry from the CFR printout if held); G.2 (every held firearm, the same rows as the battery table); G.54 to G.60 (association, accreditation number, membership number, joined and expiry, s 16 only); G.61 (the one-line motivation from Part 4.4); G.62 to G.67 (the six history questions as answered); G.68 to G.69 (safe type and mounting). Sections A, B, C, H (signature, photo, fingerprints), I, J, K and L are never pre-filled.

## Part 10. The applicant's paperwork: what to bring and what happens at the counter

This part is printed as the last page(s) of the pack under the heading "Take these with you" and is removed before the pack is handed over. It is written to the applicant in the second person. It is route-aware (dealer, private sale, estate) and section-aware.

### 10.1 Bring, for every application

1. This pack, printed single-sided, unsigned SAPS form on top, motivation and PAJA letter signed and every page of the motivation initialled.
2. Your original green ID book or smart ID card. The copy at Annexure A must already be certified, or have it certified at the station before you join the queue (free; the DFO can do it).
3. Your original competency certificate (card or CFR printout). Annexure B must be certified; have it done with the ID.
4. Your original proficiency Statement of Results. Annexure C likewise.
5. The originals of every licence copied into the pack, your association papers, the safe invoice if you have it, and anything else copied into the pack. The DFO may want to see the original of any copy.
6. Two recent colour passport photographs, plain background, your name and ID number written on the back (the form takes one at section H; stations ask for two, so bring two).
7. Proof of residential address under three months old, in your own name (a municipal account or bank letter is better than a rental statement).
8. A black pen. The SAPS form must be completed and signed in black ink, by you, in front of the DFO. Do not sign it at home.
9. The application fee, paid at the station (cash or bank-guaranteed cheque under regulation 96; most stations take cash) against a remittance advice; attach the receipt to the pack (regulation 13(6)). Confirm the current fee before you go (it is charged per firearm, and it changes).
10. Your fingerprints are taken at the counter by the DFO on the SAPS fingerprint form. That is why nobody can lodge this for you.
11. Ask for the acknowledgement of receipt (SAPS 523) and write down the reference number, the date and the DFO's name. The DFO may only issue it once the application is complete, so an application sent back is one that was not accepted.
12. Keep a full copy of everything you hand in.

### 10.2 Bring, by route

Dealer purchase: the dealer's SAPS 350(a) stock return with sections E and F of the SAPS 271 completed and stamped by the dealer, and the dealer's invoice. The firearm stays with the dealer until the licence is issued.

Private sale: the seller's signed consent (the firearm-source annexure), copies of both sides of the seller's licence and of the seller's ID, and the seller's completed section F (F.1.2 type A, F.3 to F.15 and F.79 to F.87) on the SAPS 271. The firearm stays with the seller until the licence is issued.

Estate: the deceased's licence and ID copies, the death certificate, the executor's letter of appointment, the executor's letter authorising you to apply for this firearm, the executor's ID; the executor completes section F as type E.

### 10.3 Bring, by section

s 13: nothing further. Be ready to tell the DFO the distance to your nearest police station and neighbours and to describe your routine; the DFO may complete section K on the strength of it even though it is a s 14 section.

s 14: expect the DFO to complete section K with you (urban or rural, distances, crime-rated area, high-risk work or residence, how many firearms you hold) and to inspect the premises.

s 15: your farm letter or invitation, permits and club papers in the pack; your association membership card if you are a member.

s 16: your original association membership certificate, dedicated status certificate, the chairperson's sworn statement or letter of good standing (original, as the association issued it), and the association's endorsement for this firearm by serial. Make sure your annual activity report to the association is submitted; the association will not endorse without it and the DFO may ask.

s 24: the original licence being renewed; lodge at least 90 days before its expiry; if the competency certificate runs with this licence, the competency renewal form (SAPS 517(g) or its current equivalent) with its sworn declaration is lodged at the same time. Keep the acknowledgement of receipt: it is your proof that the licence stays valid while the renewal is decided.

### 10.4 The safe

Your safe must conform to SABS 953-1 / 953-2 (regulation 86) and hold the firearm and its ammunition; the Registrar may only issue a licence to someone whose safe custody complies (regulation 13(12)). In practice the DFO inspects the safe before the licence is issued (SAPS 271 section L.1.2) and expects it to be bolted through the wall or floor (SAPS 271 item G.69). Some stations ask you to sign a safe-storage statement on their own form; sign it there. Have the three photographs in the pack and expect a visit.

### 10.5 Where and who

Only the DFO for the police station serving the area where you ordinarily live may take the application (regulation 13(4)(a) read with regulation 1). Take it in person. Verification working hours are set by the station; phone ahead.

### 10.6 Warnings printed when they apply (Part 3.2)

Each warning is printed in a box on this page, in plain words, with the section that gives rise to it and the alternative. The s 15 membership note reads: "You are a member of an accredited association and are applying under section 15 as an occasional hunter or sports shooter. The Act's definition of 'occasional' was written before associations began endorsing section 15 applications for members, and DFOs accept them, but the definition has not been changed. Your motivation describes you as a member and does not claim dedicated status. If you hold dedicated status, section 16 is the stronger route."

## Part 11. Release checks the generator runs

The pack is not released to the applicant until every deterministic check passes. Failures go back to the writer (a rewrite with the failure named) or to the applicant (a missing fact) or to an operator (a vocabulary or structure fault the applicant cannot fix), never to the applicant as a question they cannot answer.

Identity and firearm: full names, ID number, make, model, calibre and serial present verbatim in the body, on the cover, on the PAJA letter, in the index and on the form; the same ID number and names on the form's G.6 to G.9.

Section: the section applied under appears in the introduction, the statutory section, the request, the cover, the PAJA letter and the form's section D, and they agree; a renewal names the underlying section and the licence number.

Held firearms: every section stated for a held firearm matches its card; no purpose is stated for a firearm with none supplied; no banned role word; the battery table, the body sentences and SAPS 271 G.2 have the same rows and serials; a serial in the body that is neither the applied-for serial nor a held serial is a fabrication.

Competency: number and endorsement present; the endorsement covers the type and action applied for; no expiry date in prose; SAPS 271 G.1 ticks agree.

Association (s 16; and s 15 where the applicant is a member): name, accreditation number and membership number agree between the body and the annexures, and for s 16 with G.54 to G.60; "dedicated" absent from a s 15 document; the sworn statement annexed for s 16.

Annexures: every citation resolves to a letter in the index with the exact title; every indexed annexure has pages; the printed order matches the index; page numbers in the contents match the merged document; the certification block is present on A, B and C only; no annexure contains the service's name or a preview watermark.

Vocabulary: none of the Part 8.3 banned words; no Americanism; no non-South-African division; section discipline (Part 1, rule 6) holds per sentence; no outcome language anywhere including the checklist; no exclamation marks; no markdown or bullet characters in the body.

Numbers: every figure in the body traces to the fact pack; every date is after the applicant's date of birth (from the ID number) and not in the future; precinct figures match the supplied table exactly; the s 15 count sentence is arithmetically right.

Structure: the fixed headings appear in order with none added; the introduction and declaration are one paragraph; the s 13 firearm paragraph is at most 60 words; the word band for the section is met or the shortfall is explained by fewer supplied facts; the request is the last paragraph before the signature block.

Weakness flags to the applicant (not blockers): a held firearm of the same type, calibre class and section with no distinguishing reason given; a s 16 sport firearm with no named discipline; a s 13 with no precinct figures; a s 15 hunting application with no farm letter, permit or past hunt; a s 16 with no activity evidence; a semi-automatic shotgun over five shots under s 16; a s 15 handgun where one is already held.

## Part 12. Common failure modes, and the rule that prevents each

Learned from lodged packs and from generated drafts. Listed so nobody relearns them.

1. Pasting product copy into a self-defence letter ("short-recoil, tilting barrel, polymer frame, high magazine capacities, terminal ballistics"). A Registrar reads it as appetite, not need. Rule: Part 5.1 brief 5, sixty words, four facts.
2. Inventing licence sections and roles for held firearms from a count ("five firearms held") with no rows; one of them was wrong against the licence copy in the same pack. Rule: Part 3.3 item 6, rows never counts; Part 6.5.
