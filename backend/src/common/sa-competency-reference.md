**Version 3.0 — verified 25 August 2026.** Supersedes v2.

Authoritative reference for South African firearm competency certificates under the **Firearms Control Act 60 of 2000 (FCA)**, as amended (most recently by the Domestic Violence Amendment Act 14 of 2021, commenced 14 April 2023).

Scope: competency types, SAPS card abbreviations, validity periods, renewal mechanics. Factual reference, not legal advice.

## 1. What competency is

A competency certificate is applied for under **section 9** and issued/specified under **section 10** of the FCA. It is a prerequisite for a firearm licence but is **not itself a licence**. It certifies that the holder is a fit and proper person and has completed the prescribed knowledge test and practical handling tests. **[ACT]**

Two things are required before a person may lawfully possess a firearm — section 3(1): **[ACT]**

1. A valid competency certificate covering that category, and
2. A valid licence, permit or authorisation for that specific firearm, under section **13, 14, 15, 16, 16A, 17, 19 or 20** (plus section 12 additional licences, and section 21 temporary authorisations).

> **Correction from v2.** v2 listed "13, 14, 15, 16, 16(2), 17 or 20". There is no licensing section 16(2). The missing sections are **16A (professional hunting, in force 1 March 2012)** and **19 (public collection)**. **[ACT]**

**The one exception: muzzle loading firearms.** Section 3(2): *"No person may possess a muzzle loading firearm unless he or she has been issued with the relevant competency certificate."* No licence — competency alone. **[ACT]**

Forms: application **SAPS 517**; further competency **SAPS 517(a)**; renewal **SAPS 517(g)**. **[SAPS]**

## 2. Competency categories

### 2.1 The statutory categories

Section 9(1) and section 10(1)(a) list **six** competency types, and only these: **[ACT]**

| # | Competency to… | FCA ref |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | possess a firearm | s10(1)(a)(i) |
| 2 | possess a **muzzle loading firearm** | s10(1)(a)(iA) |
| 3 | possess a firearm **as a private collector**, in the prescribed category | s10(1)(a)(iB) |
| 4 | **trade** in firearms (dealer) | s10(1)(a)(ii) |
| 5 | **manufacture** firearms | s10(1)(a)(iii) |
| 6 | conduct business as a **gunsmith** | s10(1)(a)(iv) |

> **Correction from v2.** v2 listed a *"competency to conduct business in hunting"*. **This does not exist.** "Business in hunting" is a **licence** purpose under s20(2)(e), not a competency category. Categories 2 and 3 above were missing from v2 entirely. **[ACT]**

**Private collector category (s10(1)(a)(iB), Regulation 14(4)):** the Registrar must specify on the certificate the category in which the accredited collectors' association has classified the member, and *"may only licence a firearm to a private collector in accordance with the category reflected on the competency certificate."* Category upgrades require the certificate to be altered. **[ACT]**

Any natural person trading on behalf of a dealer must personally hold the relevant competency certificate — e.g. Regulation 23(e) (temporary authorisation): *"only persons specified in the temporary authorisation and who has in his or her possession a relevant competency certificate, may trade at the specified premises on behalf of the dealer"*. **[ACT — Regulations]**

### 2.2 Firearm-type endorsements (possession competency)

**Important scoping note.** Neither the Act nor the 2004 Regulations prescribe firearm-type endorsements on a possession competency certificate. The words *"carbine"*, *"self-loading"* and *"manually operated"* appear **nowhere** in the Act or the Regulations. The type-endorsement system is **entirely SAPS administrative practice**, derived from which SAQA unit standards the applicant completed. **[ACT — by absence]**

What SAPS's own **SAPS 271** licence application form actually asks for is a three-way split: **[SAPS]**

> Section F.1.4 — *"Competency certificate to possess a firearm (Indicate with an X): **Handgun / Rifle / Shotgun**"*

and separately, for the firearm itself:

> Section E.1 — Type: *"Rifle / Shotgun / Handgun / Combination / Other (armament/indeterminable design type)"*
> Section E.1.1 — Action: *"Semi-automatic / Automatic / Manual / Other action (specify)"*

Endorsements as commonly issued and captured in practice: **[PRACTICE]**

| Endorsement (as commonly written) | Description | Unit standard |
|---|---|---|
| Handgun | Pistol or revolver | 119649 |
| Rifle or carbine, manually operated | Bolt, lever, pump, single-shot | 119651 |
| Rifle or carbine, self-loading | Semi-automatic rifle/carbine | 119650 |
| Shotgun | All shotgun actions | 119652 |
| Muzzle loading firearm | Black powder | 243200 |

> **Correction from v2.** v2 split handgun into "non-self-loading (revolver)" and "self-loading (pistol)", and split shotgun into "manually operated" and "self-loading", as four separate endorsements. **There is no separate unit standard for a self-loading shotgun, nor for a self-loading handgun.** 119649 covers handguns; 119652 covers shotguns. The only action-type split with a training basis behind it is **rifle/carbine: 119651 (manual) vs 119650 (self-loading)**. **[Verified against SAQA]**
>
> Handgun endorsements *are* sometimes written on cards as `N/S/L HG` / `S/L HG`, and shotgun sometimes as `M/O SG` / `S/L SG`. Where that appears, treat it as data-capture convention, not as a distinct competency — but see §4.7: if the CFR record says it, the CFR record governs the licence application. **[PRACTICE]**

### 2.3 Business competency

Trade, manufacture and gunsmith competency are separate applications with their own unit standards (the 1235xx "for business purposes" series) and their own validity, tied to the underlying business licence. **[ACT]**

### 4.7 Compound endorsements and data-capture errors

A block such as `S/L-RIFLE/CARB/PIST CAL CARB` reads as competency to possess **self-loading rifles/carbines and self-loading pistol calibre carbines**, from unit standard 119650. **[PRACTICE]**

`PIST CAL CARB` is spelled out because it is commonly misread. A 9mm AR-pattern carbine, CZ Scorpion, Kel-Tec Sub2000 and similar fire a handgun cartridge but are treated as carbines, falling under rifle/carbine competency, **not** handgun competency.

> **Correction from v2.** v2 stated: *"Barrel length and overall length drive the classification, not calibre."* **There is no statutory basis for this.** The FCA defines *"handgun"* (s1: *"a pistol or revolver which can be held in and discharged with one hand"*) but defines neither *"rifle"*, *"carbine"* nor *"shotgun"*. The word *"carbine"* does not appear in the Act or the 2004 Regulations at all. The PCC-as-carbine treatment is CFR/SAPS administrative practice and the criteria are not published. **[UNVERIFIED]** — the practical conclusion (get rifle/carbine competency for a PCC) still holds, but do not state a length threshold as if it were law.

**Caution — unchanged and still correct.** SAPS data capturers are inconsistent. The same endorsement appears written out in full on some certificates and abbreviated on others, and typos occur. **Any discrepancy must be corrected at the DFO before a licence application is lodged**, because the licence application is validated against the CFR competency record, not against the piece of paper. **[PRACTICE]**

## 5. Validity and expiry

### 5.1 The governing rule

Section 10(2), as substituted by section 9(b) of Act 28 of 2006 with effect from **10 January 2011**: **[ACT — verbatim]**

> *"A competency certificate contemplated in subsection (1)(a)(i), (ii), (iii) and (iv), remains valid for the same period of validity as the period determined in this Act in respect of the licence to which the competency certificate relates, unless the competency certificate is terminated or renewed in accordance with the provisions of this Act."*

SAPS restates this verbatim on its own FAQ. **[SAPS]**

Three things follow that v2 did not flag:

1. **s10(2) does not cover all six competency types.** It applies to (i) possess a firearm, (ii) trade, (iii) manufacture, (iv) gunsmith. It does **not** cover **(iA) muzzle loading** — which has its own rule, s10(3) — and it does **not** cover **(iB) private collector**. The validity of a private-collector competency is a genuine statutory gap. **[ACT — by omission]**
2. The section says *"the same period of validity as the **period** determined in this Act"*, and *"the licence"* — **singular**. On a literal reading it aligns the *duration* (5 years / 10 years) rather than mandating a maximum across a portfolio of licences.
3. **The "maximum across all licences in the category" model is an interpretation, not the statutory text.** It is what SAPS does and what the whole industry teaches, but it is not what s10(2) says. See §5.3.

### 5.2 Does the certificate carry an expiry date?

> **Major correction from v2.** v2 stated emphatically: *"A competency certificate does not carry an expiry date… Never parse an expiry date off a competency certificate. There isn't one."* **This is overstated and, for many certificates, simply wrong.**
>
> **SAPS's own SAPS 271 form, Section F, fields 1.6 and 1.7, requires the applicant to enter the competency certificate's "Date of issue" AND its "Expiry date".** SAPS would not ask for a field that does not exist. **[SAPS]**

The accurate position:

- **Certificates and cards do frequently carry a printed expiry date.** Pre-10-January-2011 certificates were issued under the original flat 5-year regime and carry a 5-year expiry on their face.
- **That printed date is not legally determinative.** After 10 January 2011, s10(2) decoupled validity from the certificate. SAGA's guidance at the time: certificates valid on 10 January 2011 had their expiry *"automatically extended to expire on the same date as your longest relevant existing firearm licence."* **[PRACTICE]**
- Consequently a printed date and the CFR position can disagree — and industry sources state plainly that SAPS may link the competency to the longest valid licence *"even though there may be a different expiry date on the competency."* **[PRACTICE]**

**Operational rule for a platform (revised):** read the printed expiry date if there is one, but **treat it as advisory input, never as the answer**. The determinative position is the CFR record, confirmed via the DFO. Do not build a hard gate on either a printed date or a computed date — see §8.

### 5.3 The derived-expiry model

This is the model SAPS applies and the industry teaches. **[PRACTICE — well-supported, universally followed, not officially published]**

```
competency_expiry(type) ≈ MAX(expiry_date of every current licence in that type)
```

NATSHOOT states it directly: one's competency for a category of firearm *"expires on the date of the longest outstanding licence of that category of firearm one owns."* SA Hunters refers to a SAPS directive to the same effect. NATSHOOT further cites a National Commissioner's Directive of 3 February 2016 on renewals.

**I could not retrieve the directive text itself, and no court judgment or published CFR circular on the point surfaced.** The derivation is sound and universally followed; it is not officially documented in anything verifiable. **[UNVERIFIED as to primary source]**

Consequences of the model:

- Each endorsement on a single certificate can have a **different** derived expiry, computed independently per firearm type.
- The date is **not fixed at issue**. It moves.
- The certificate is never reissued when the underlying licences change. Only the CFR record reflects the current position.

**Rolling expiry.** Because the expiry is the maximum across licences in the category, it rolls forward whenever a new licence is obtained in that category, or an existing one in that category is renewed. An owner actively acquiring or renewing within a category may never face a standalone competency renewal. The competency comes under threat only when the *last remaining* licence in that category approaches expiry with nothing behind it.

**Worked example** (using corrected licence periods from §5.4)

| Event | Licences in "handgun" | Derived competency expiry |
|---|---|---|
| Handgun competency issued 2020 | none | ~2025 — *see the warning below* |
| s13 handgun licensed 2021, 5-yr | 2026 | 2026 |
| s15 sport handgun licensed 2023, 10-yr | 2026, 2033 | 2033 |
| s13 handgun renewed 2026, 5-yr | 2031, 2033 | 2033 (unchanged, not the max) |
| s15 handgun renewed 2033, 10-yr | 2031, 2043 | 2043 |

### 5.3.1 The no-licence case — v2 overstated this

> **Correction from v2.** v2 asserted a hard rule: *"If no licence has ever been issued in that type… the fallback is 5 years from the competency issue date."* **No official source supports this.** s10(2) is circular where no licence exists — validity is tied to *"the licence to which the competency certificate relates"*, and there isn't one.
>
> What is actually true: **SAPS practice varies, and it is often treated as 5 years.** The most candid industry sources say exactly that, and training providers tell holders to phone their DFO to find out — which is itself evidence that the answer lives in the CFR system, not in a published rule. **[UNVERIFIED]**

Treat the 5-year figure as a **planning assumption**, not a rule. Never present it to a user as the legal position.

### 5.4 Licence periods feeding the calculation — CORRECTED

Section 27, substituted by section 18 of Act 28 of 2006 with effect from **10 January 2011**: **[ACT — verbatim from the Table]**

| Section | Type of licence or permit | Period |
|---|---|---|
| 13 | Self-defence | **Five years** |
| 14 | Restricted firearm for self-defence | **Two years** |
| 15 | Occasional hunting and sports-shooting | **10 years** |
| 16 | Dedicated hunting and dedicated sports-shooting | **10 years** |
| 16A | Professional hunting | **10 years** |
| 17 | Private collection | **10 years** |
| 18 | Permit — ammunition in private collection | **10 years** |
| 19 | Public collection (licence and ammunition permit) | **10 years** |
| 20 | Business purposes: **business as game rancher and in hunting** | **10 years** |
| 20 | Business purposes: **other than as game rancher and in hunting** | **Five years** |

> **Correction from v2 — and a trap worth understanding.** v2 said: *"S20 business in hunting 5 yrs; other/security/training/film/game rancher 2 yrs."* **That is the pre-2011 position.** The current section 27 Table gives game rancher **and** hunting **10 years**, and all other business purposes **5 years**.
>
> The reason v2 got this wrong is instructive: **the SAPS 271 form still prints the old table.** Section D.3 of the form in circulation lists "Business in hunting — Five years", "Other business purposes — Two years", "As a game rancher — Two years". The form has not been updated to reflect a change that took effect on 10 January 2011. It also omits section 16A entirely (its header reads *"Section 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 19 and 20"*). **Where the SAPS form and the Act disagree, the Act governs — but expect DFO friction.** **[ACT vs SAPS conflict]**
>
> v2 also omitted s16A and s18.

**Planning implication (unchanged):** the licence section chosen for a firearm determines how far the competency expiry is pushed out. Where there is a genuine choice, the longer-period section extends the whole category.

### 5.5 Muzzle loaders — the standalone case

Muzzle loading firearms require **no licence** (s3(2)) — competency alone. There is therefore no licence to inherit from, and the Act gives this competency its own fixed lifespan: **[ACT]**

> **Section 10(3):** *"A competency certificate relating to a muzzle loading firearm lapses after **ten years from its date of issue**, unless the competency certificate is terminated or renewed in accordance with the provisions of this Act."*

Section 10A(2) requires the holder to apply for renewal **at least 90 days before the expiry of the competency certificate itself**.

> **Correction from v2.** v2 identified the standalone case and the 90-day rule correctly but **omitted the ten-year period from s10(3)** — the single most concrete validity number in the whole Act.

Statutory definition (s1, inserted by Act 28 of 2006): a muzzle loading firearm fires only a single shot per barrel, requires reloading through the muzzle with separate components (measured black powder charge, wad, and lead bullet/sabot/shot), ignited with flint, match, wheel or percussion cap. **[ACT]**

### 5.6 Private collector competency — a genuine gap

s10(2) does not extend to s10(1)(a)(iB). s10(3) covers only muzzle loaders. **Nothing in the Act states how long a private-collector competency remains valid.** In practice it is presumably aligned to the s17 licence (10 years), but this is inference. **Verify with the CFR. [UNVERIFIED]**

### 5.7 Business competency

Follows the inherit-from-the-licence principle in s10(2), tied to the underlying business licence. Dealer, manufacturer and gunsmith licence periods are governed by Chapter 7, not by the s27 Table. **Verify directly with the CFR before relying on a specific number.** **[UNVERIFIED]**

## 6. Renewal mechanics

### 6.1 The 90-day rules — two different provisions

> **Correction from v2.** v2 presented a single general "90-day rule" and cited s10A(6) as the general protection. **The Act does not work that way.** There are two separate provisions and they cover different things. **[ACT]**

**For a firearm licence — section 24:**
- s24(1): the holder *"must at least 90 days before the date of expiry of the licence apply to the Registrar for its renewal."*
- s24(4): *"If an application for the renewal of a licence has been lodged within the period provided for in subsection (1), the licence remains valid until the application is decided."*

**For a competency certificate — section 10A:**
- s10A(1): a holder who wishes to renew *"must apply in the prescribed form to the Registrar for its renewal **together with an application for the renewal of the licence to which the competency certificate relates**."* — no independent 90-day figure is stated here; it inherits the licence's timing.
- s10A(2): a **muzzle loading** competency holder *"must apply at least 90 days before the date of expiry of the competency certificate."*
- s10A(6): *"If an application for the renewal of a competency certificate has been lodged **within the period provided for in subsection (2)**, the competency certificate remains valid until the application is decided."*

**The nuance that matters:** s10A(6) is drafted by reference to **subsection (2) — the muzzle-loading provision**. On a literal reading, the "remains valid pending decision" protection for competency certificates is anchored to the muzzle-loading timeline. For an ordinary possession competency the practical protection comes from s24(4) applying to the licence it is lodged with. **[ACT — literal reading]**

> **Also corrected:** v2 said the s10A(6) protection runs *"including through any appeal"*. **The Act does not say this.** SAPS's own renewal FAQ does say a timeously-renewed licence stays valid pending the outcome including appeal — so the proposition is right in substance for **licences**, but it is a SAPS statement, not statutory text, and v2 attributed it to the wrong provision. **[SAPS, not ACT]**

**Bottom line for users:** lodge **at least 90 days before expiry**, every time, for both. That advice is unchanged and correct.

### 6.2 Competency renews with the licence — restated accurately

> **Correction from v2.** v2 said competency *"renews as part of the licence renewal"*. **Half true, and the half that's wrong matters.** They are **two separate applications, on two separate forms, with two separate fees.** What the law does is *align the expiry dates*, which makes simultaneous lodgement the natural workflow. **[ACT + SAPS]**

- s10A(1) requires the competency renewal to be lodged **together with** the licence renewal — it does **not** make it automatic.
- SAPS's renewal guidance says applicants must hold a valid, relevant competency and **"may need to renew it using the SAPS 517(g) form"** — *may*, not *must always*. **[SAPS]**
- NATSHOOT: *"one may hand in an application for a renewal of a licence and for the renewal of the relevant competency at the same time."* **[PRACTICE]**
- gunlicence.co.za: *"If you do not have additional valid licences in the same class of firearm, it is safe to assume that your competency certificate also needs to be renewed."* **[PRACTICE]**

**So: a SAPS 517(g) is required only when the competency is actually expiring** — i.e. when the licence being renewed is your longest-dated licence in that firearm class. If a longer-dated licence exists in the same class, the competency runs to that later date and no 517(g) is due.

Note also: a **new** competency and a **new** licence cannot be lodged together. Competency must be approved first. **[PRACTICE]**

**If the licence has already expired**, the renewal route is closed. It becomes a new licence application (SAPS 271), and a valid competency must already be in hand. The firearm is unlicensed in the interim and — per industry guidance — cannot be renewed, sold to a dealer, sold privately, or given away to anyone other than SAPS. **What happens to the competency itself in that scenario is not addressed by any source found. [UNVERIFIED]**

**A standalone competency renewal is the exception, not the norm.** A standalone 517(g) is needed when:

- The competency was issued but no licence in that type was ever applied for (the assumed ~5-year fallback is approaching — see §5.3.1), or
- The last remaining licence in that category is expiring and is not being renewed or replaced, or
- The licence in that category has already lapsed, breaking the inheritance chain, or
- It is a **muzzle loading** competency (always standalone — s10(3) fixed 10 years).

### 6.3 No retraining on renewal — confirmed

**Section 10A(7)** (verbatim): *"For the purposes of the renewal of a competency certificate an applicant does not have to comply again with the provisions of section 9(2)(q) and (r), unless the Registrar in a specific case, on good grounds being present, requires otherwise."* **[ACT]**

(q) is the knowledge test, (r) is the practical handling test. **This is the practically important provision in s10A and v2 stated it correctly.**

Regulation 14(9) makes the analogous point for a **further** competency: an applicant who submits proof of previously completed training and practical tests need not repeat them. **[ACT]**

### 6.4 What a renewal application requires

Statutory minimum — **s10A(3)**: **[ACT]**
- Application in the prescribed form (SAPS 517(g))
- A declaration under oath or solemn statement confirming the applicant still conforms to s9(2)
- Delivered to the DFO for the area where the applicant ordinarily resides or where the business is situated

In practice DFOs also require: **[SAPS/PRACTICE]**
- Two certified copies of ID or passport (permanent residence permit for non-citizens)
- Copy of the current competency certificate, front and back
- Certified copy of the PFTC Statement of Results (not strictly required — s10A(7) — but often requested)
- Proof of address not older than 3 months
- Three character references (typically one family member, two friends/colleagues)
- Two full-colour passport photographs
- Three photographs of the safe: door closed, door open, and the mounting bolts (not a requirement for competency, but commonly requested)
- Full set of fingerprints taken by the DFO on **SAPS 91(a)**
- Prescribed fee, paid via **SAPS 523(a)** remittance advice; keep the receipt

## 7. Failure modes and hard blocks

| Condition | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Competency expired (derived or CFR date passed) | Cannot renew any licence in that firearm type. Cannot lodge a new licence application in that type. |
| Last licence in a category lapses without replacement | Inheritance chain breaks. Competency for that type falls back to whatever SAPS practice applies (often treated as 5 years from issue, which has usually long passed). Standalone 517(g) required. |
| Licence expired | Renewal route closed. New SAPS 271 required, valid competency a precondition. Firearm is unlicensed in the interim and cannot lawfully be sold or transferred to anyone but SAPS. |
| Renewal lodged fewer than 90 days before expiry | s24(4) protection does not apply to the licence. Expect the licence to lapse on its expiry date regardless of a pending application. |
| Endorsement wrong or mis-captured on the card or in the CFR | Licence application fails validation against the CFR. Correct at the DFO first. |
| Muzzle loading competency > 10 years old | Lapsed under s10(3). Possession of the muzzle loader becomes unlawful — there is no licence layer to fall back on. |

### 7.1 Section-level restrictions — CORRECTED

Verified directly against the Act. **[ACT]**

| Section | What may be licensed | Verbatim basis |
|---|---|---|
| **s13** self-defence | *"any shotgun which is **not fully or semi-automatic**; or handgun which is not fully automatic"* | s13(1) |
| **s14** restricted self-defence | *"any semi-automatic rifle or shotgun, which cannot readily be converted into a fully automatic firearm"*, or a firearm declared restricted by the Minister | s14(1) |
| **s15** occasional | *"handgun which is not fully automatic; rifle or shotgun which is **not fully or semi-automatic**"* — and not a restricted firearm | s15(1) |
| **s16** dedicated | *"handgun which is not fully automatic; rifle or shotgun which is not fully automatic; **semi-automatic shotgun manufactured to fire no more than five shots in succession without having to be reloaded**"* | s16(1) |
| **s16A** professional hunting | handgun / rifle / shotgun not fully automatic | s16A(1) |

> **Correction from v2 — this one could cause real harm.** v2 stated: *"A self-loading shotgun is the exception in the self-loading group: it can be licensed under S13."* **This is flatly wrong.** Section 13(1)(a) permits only a shotgun *"which is **not** fully or semi-automatic"*. A semi-automatic shotgun is expressly a **restricted firearm** under s14(1)(a) and must be licensed under **s14 (two years)** or, if it is manufactured to fire no more than five shots without reloading, under **s16 (ten years)** with dedicated status.

Also correct in v2 and confirmed: a self-loading rifle or carbine cannot go under s13 or s15; it must go under **s16** (or s14 for self-defence, or s16A), requiring dedicated hunter / dedicated sport shooter status through an accredited association — **s16(2)** requires a sworn statement from the chairperson of an accredited association confirming registered membership.

### 7.2 Licence-count limits v2 omitted

**[ACT]**

- **s13(3):** no person may hold more than **one** s13 licence.
- **s14(5):** no person may hold more than **one** s14 licence.
- **s15(3):** no more than **four** s15 licences; reduced to **three** if the person also holds a s13 licence; no more than **one** s15 handgun; and the count is further reduced by any s12 additional licences held.
- **s11:** a separate licence is required for each firearm.

These are hard constraints on what a buyer can actually license, and belong in any eligibility logic.

## 8. Marketplace / platform implications

- Buyer must hold **valid competency for the correct firearm category** before a transfer can proceed, and must separately obtain the licence for that specific firearm under the applicable section.
- **Type matching matters, but use the categories that actually exist.** SAPS 271 splits competency three ways — handgun / rifle / shotgun — with the rifle side further split by action (manual vs self-loading) through the unit standards. A handgun endorsement does not cover a pistol calibre carbine. Validate against firearm classification, not calibre.
- **Do not treat a printed expiry date as authoritative, and do not treat its absence as meaning there is none.** Both occur. §5.2.
- **Do not treat a computed expiry as authoritative either.** The max-licence-expiry model is well-supported practice with no verifiable primary source, and the no-licence case has no rule at all. §5.3, §5.3.1.
- **Recommended design:** store competency expiry as a **user-supplied, user-editable, advisory field** with a "confirm with your DFO" note. Drive any hard gate off the **licence** expiry, which is unambiguous and printed. Where a competency check is genuinely required, require sight of the certificate without the platform adjudicating its expiry.
- Any stored `competency_expiry` is a **cached derivation**, not a fact. Recompute whenever a linked licence is added, renewed or lapses. Invalidatable state, not an immutable field.
- Derived expiry differs per endorsement on the same certificate. Validate the specific endorsement.
- **Section eligibility and count limits constrain what a buyer can license.** A semi-auto rifle listing is only actionable by a buyer with s16 (or s14/s16A) standing. A semi-auto shotgun listing is **not** actionable under s13 — it needs s14 or s16. A buyer who already holds a s13 licence can hold only three s15 licences, and only one s15 handgun.

## 9. Known ambiguity and open questions

Ranked by how likely they are to bite.

1. **The max-licence-expiry rule has no verifiable primary source.** SAPS applies it, NATSHOOT and SA Hunters teach it, a National Commissioner's Directive of 3 February 2016 is cited for it — but the directive text could not be retrieved and no judgment or published CFR circular states it. **[UNVERIFIED]**
2. **No rule at all for a competency with no licence behind it.** Practice varies; "5 years" is a common default, not a rule. **[UNVERIFIED]**
3. **No rule for private-collector competency validity.** s10(2) does not cover s10(1)(a)(iB) and s10(3) covers only muzzle loaders. **[UNVERIFIED]**
4. **s10A(6) is anchored to s10A(2), the muzzle-loading provision.** Whether SAPS reads it as generally applicable is unknown. **[UNVERIFIED]**
5. **SAPS's own SAPS 271 form contradicts s27 on business licence periods** and omits s16A. Expect DFO friction where the Act and the form differ. **[ACT vs SAPS conflict]**
6. **Unit standard 117705's last-enrolment date (30 June 2026) has passed** with no replacement registered. **[Verified as at Aug 2026; status may have changed]**
7. **The PFTC / QCTO / SASSETA accreditation dispute is unresolved**, preserved by an interim court order of 11 July 2024 confirmed in ZAGPPHC 795 (1 August 2025). No 2026 outcome found. **[UNVERIFIED beyond Aug 2025]**
8. **No 2026/27 SAPS fee schedule found.** **[UNVERIFIED]**
9. **Certificates issued 2004–2011 remain the messiest.** The Act 28 of 2006 provisions commenced on **10 January 2011**, so pre-commencement certificates were issued under the original flat 5-year regime and may carry a printed 5-year expiry that was superseded by the s10(2) linkage.
10. **Training providers routinely say "some competencies are 5 years and some are 10, check yours."** This is a rough restatement of the derived rule, communicated as if the period were fixed at issue. It is not.
11. **The CFR record is authoritative.** It gates the licence application. Where a calculated date, a printed date and the CFR disagree, **the CFR wins.** Confirm through the DFO before relying on any date for a compliance decision.
